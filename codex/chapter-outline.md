## The Knowledge-Gating Discipline (Author Knowledge vs. Page Knowledge)

### 19.1 The Knowledge-Gating Discipline (Author Knowledge vs. Page Knowledge)

🟢 The single most important discipline for drafting from a bible this deep: the bible knows about the Erosion, the adopted ontological reveal, sympathetic terrain, Gate classification, institutional theories, and the eventual geopolitical weight of Null Passage. Kasane, on any given page, should know almost none of it. A model drafting from this document will naturally try to be "helpful" and leak explanations early — this section exists to stop that.

Every chapter prompt going forward should carry four explicit fields:
- **Kasane knows:** — facts she has directly confirmed.
- **Kasane suspects:** — patterns she's noticed but hasn't verified (ties to her rumor-evaluation method, Section 5 old numbering / Section 18's core intellectual flaw).
- **Kasane is wrong about:** — at least one live, specific misapprehension per chapter where possible, not a vague fog of ignorance.
- **Kasane cannot know yet:** — author-only facts explicitly off-limits for this chapter, regardless of how naturally they'd explain what's happening.

This operationalizes Section 1.15's information-reveal rule (symptom → pattern → competing explanations → new evidence → partial model → model failure → deeper model) at the chapter level rather than leaving it as a saga-wide abstraction.

## Chapter-Prompt Template

### 19.4 Chapter-Prompt Template

🟢 Prompts should specify outcomes, not choreography — a prompt that blocks out "she walks to the kiln, touches the vent, hears a crack, turns left" produces stiff prose because the drafting model executes instructions instead of discovering the scene. A prompt that states "her craft knowledge must eliminate the mundane explanation before the impossible becomes unavoidable" leaves room for the scene to live. Target length: roughly 500–900 words for a complex chapter, shorter for a simple one — deliberately not prose-heavy pre-writing.

Fields, in order: **POV / location / time** → **opening image** → **immediate want** → **scene problem** → **information state** (the four fields from Section 19.1) → **character pressure** (which recurring relationship, if any, is live in this scene, and what it's testing) → **required beats** (outcomes, not staging) → **forbidden reveals** (explicit author-only facts this chapter must not leak) → **consequence created** (feeds Section 1.16's Consequence Ledger) → **closing image/turn** → **craft reminders** (pointer to the specific Section 1/8/18 rules most likely to matter for this particular scene, not the whole rulebook restated).

## Chapter 1 — Refinements

### 19.5 Chapter 1 — Refinements (not yet drafted; this section prepares the prompt, does not write the prose)

🟢 **No monster fight needed.** The Gate/Marking event itself is sufficient; adding a proper combat beat would dilute rather than strengthen the chapter unless drafting reveals an actual gap.

**Structural progression for the chapter:** competence → discrepancy → investigation → impossible event → survival → bodily wrongness → responsibility → descent → social recognition. The chapter opens with a problem happening *to* Kasane and closes with Kasane herself becoming a problem other people must interpret — that inversion is the whole chapter's job.

**Correction to an earlier instinct:** don't artificially delay Kasane recognizing a Rent/Gate — she's a Yamashiro adult, she should know the category quickly. The mystery isn't "what is this," it's "why isn't this behaving like what I know it to be." Her competence should never be withheld just to manufacture reader ignorance; withholding what *she* would obviously know reads as false, not mysterious.

**The ending beat (locked as the target, three options were weighed):**
- *Option A (rejected):* the eyes alone — someone sees her and everything stops. Too purely visual, not enough character mechanism.
- *Option B (chosen):* **the report inversion.** Kasane spends the entire descent thinking the urgent problem is *what's on the mountain*. She reaches town intending to report a Rent. The person she reports to is staring at her instead — the town already thinks the urgent problem is *what came down from it*. This is character-driven rather than interface-driven, and launches Movement II naturally.
- *Option C (folded in, not standalone):* a restrained System intrusion can sit earlier in the chapter, but shouldn't be the closing beat.

**System-text budget for Chapter 1: exactly two intrusions.** First, recognition (the Marking registers). Second, failure (it can't classify her). Then the System goes quiet for the rest of the chapter — per Section 1.9/8.15's rule against overuse, the reader should want a third message and not get one.

**What Chapter 1 is allowed to sell, and nothing more:** Kasane as a person; Yamashiro feeling real and lived-in; something impossible having happened; Kasane now implicated in it. No Clan-name inventory, no rank-ladder explanation, no Erosion name-drop, no Stitch terminology beyond what she'd actually call it in the moment. Section 17's ensemble discipline already establishes that later regions get independent lives rather than existing as extensions of Kasane — Chapter 1 doesn't need to advertise Haejin, Bailian, or any of it.

## Kasane's Flaw Must Land Early

### 19.6 Kasane's Flaw Must Land Early (extends Section 18.5, placed concretely in the spine)

🟢 Timing target: within roughly the first quarter of Arc 1 (Movement III or IV, not Chapter 1 — the flaw needs an established pattern-reading track record before it can misfire meaningfully). The failure must be specific: a reasonable, well-evidenced read on one of the recurring cast (Section 19.3) that turns out wrong — not because she missed a clue, but because that person chooses something irrational by her standards: love, humiliation, loyalty, pride, grief. This is the guardrail against the common failure mode where a protagonist's "flaw" never actually costs anything and everyone else just reads as slower than her.
