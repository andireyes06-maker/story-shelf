## Locked Saga Architecture

### Base (original locked handoff)

## 10. SAGA STRUCTURE — CONVERGENT ENSEMBLE

**Arc Structure**

```
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Phase             | Shape                                                    |
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Early arcs        | Each arc = new region, new protagonist, complete         |
|                   | character arc. Not placeholders. Fully developed before  |
|                   | convergence.                                             |
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Structural bridge | Kasane (Arc 1) is the only character type mechanically   |
|                   | able to cross regional/system boundaries others can't.   |
|                   | Organic, non-coincidental reason she becomes the         |
|                   | connective thread.                                       |
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Mid-saga          | Arcs stop being fully separate. Earlier protagonists     |
|                   | appear in later arcs' climaxes and vice versa.           |
|                   | Convergence is earned, not sudden.                       |
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Late saga         | Full shared-ensemble structure. Combined threat.         |
|                   | Found-family dynamic. Every member arrives with a fully  |
|                   | developed voice and history from their own arc.          |
+-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Protagonist Status**
Kasane is first among equals — not the sole emotional lead for the whole saga. The ensemble becomes truly shared later.

**Characters Between Arcs**
Characters from earlier arcs continue off-page development between their arc and convergence. When they reappear, they have been doing things. They are not frozen waiting for Kasane to return. Some have rebuilt standing they lost. Some have lost standing they had. The world did not wait. (Mushoku Tensei technique.)

### 17.1 The Fundamental Independence Test, and Where Protagonists Come From

🟢 A concrete test for whether Arcs 2–5's protagonists (still unnamed per Section 17H) have been developed enough once they're drafted: **if removing Kasane causes their entire personal story to stop functioning, they haven't been developed enough.** Before convergence, each needs an independent goal, an independent conflict, relationships Kasane had no role in creating, and failures Kasane cannot repair. The design starting point should never be "the ensemble needs a healer/tank/strategist" — it should be **"who could only have been produced by this civilization?"** Their eventual ensemble function should fall out of who they already are, not the reverse.

### 17.2 Arc-Specific Character Questions, and Kasane's Function in Each Arc

🟢 One thematic question per arc, sharper than the region-level pressure words already in Section 14.4 — usable directly when each arc is eventually drafted:
- **Arc 1 (Yamashiro):** *What happens when a person who wants to understand the rules discovers the rules don't describe what happened to her?* Kasane begins wanting enough understanding to return to ordinary life and ends accepting involvement, not destiny — those are different things (consistent with Section 18.15's arc movement).
- **Arc 2 (Haejin):** *What remains yours when everything valuable about you can be assigned a price?* Kasane's function here is destabilizing rather than central — Haejin asks *what can it do*, then *who controls it*, then *what is it worth*, in that order, before the Haejin protagonist becomes the arc's actual emotional center.
- **Arc 3 (Bailian):** *What happens when the institution that taught you to understand the divine encounters something it cannot explain?* Kasane doesn't need to win a theological argument — her existence alone is the evidence; the Bailian protagonist has to decide what the contradiction means, and Kasane can't decide it for them.
- **Arc 4 (Erkhaan):** *When survival genuinely requires sacrifice, who has the right to decide who gets sacrificed?* This protagonist should challenge Kasane's instinct toward individual autonomy harder than a simple authoritarian villain could, by pointing at an actual defensive line and saying "if these people leave, that city dies" — a line she can't answer with a slogan.
- **Arc 5 (the Harrowed):** *Who are you when no institution remains to tell you what you are?* For the first time, Kasane's classification (Null Passage, Stirred, clanless, anomaly) barely matters — the Harrowed care whether she keeps agreements, survives, contributes, and can be trusted, full stop.

### 17.3 Two Anti-Patterns to Actively Avoid

🟢 **"Five origins," not five arcs:** Arc 2 must not read as "meet future ensemble member #2," nor Arc 3 as "recruit the priest," nor Arc 4 as "recruit the soldier," nor Arc 5 as "party assembled." Each protagonist needs a locally complete arc — if the saga stopped after their regional story, a reader should still recognize a finished character arc.

**The "Kasane Parade":** never let the saga settle into arrives → meets regional protagonist → fixes their problem → protagonist joins Kasane → repeat. Vary it deliberately: sometimes Kasane arrives because *she* needs something; sometimes another protagonist finds her; sometimes they work against each other; sometimes Kasane leaves before the regional conflict resolves; sometimes someone else solves what Kasane couldn't; sometimes Kasane makes things worse; sometimes two protagonists meet without her present at all. The network needs to eventually function without its original connecting node.

### 17.4 Structural Bridge ≠ Social Leader

🟢 Being the mechanical connective thread (Section 14, "Kasane as structural bridge") does not mean she automatically becomes commander, diplomat, emotional center, strategist, strongest combatant, or moral authority — other ensemble members should surpass her at commanding, negotiating, theology, money, inspiration, conventional combat, Ura scholarship, or maintaining relationships. Her irreplaceable contribution is specifically the ability to connect things that are supposed to stay separate — mechanically, narratively, and eventually emotionally — not being the best at anything else.

### 17.5 Crossover Escalation, POV Ownership, and Seeing Kasane From Outside

🟢 Crossover should escalate gradually rather than waiting for every intro arc to finish: *Arc 1* — Kasane alone, other regions exist only through rumor/trade/travelers. *Arc 2* — she enters someone else's story and isn't automatically the dominant POV. *Arc 3* — Arc 2's protagonist appears briefly or influences events remotely; connections start becoming a network, not a chain. *Arc 4* — multiple earlier protagonists have reasons to get involved without necessarily traveling together. *Arc 5* — characters produced or discarded by multiple systems collide in the Harrowed, and the ensemble becomes structurally unavoidable.

POV stays one-per-scene (Section 1, locked) even as the cast grows — a scene belongs to whoever has the strongest personal stake, informational limitation, emotional contradiction, or capacity to make its meaningful decision, never simply to whoever is strongest. Increasingly important as the saga progresses: **show Kasane from outside.** From another protagonist's POV she may read as unnervingly calm, socially strange, frightening, unimpressive, or far more dangerous than she feels from inside her own head — she thinks *I need to get through that wall*; someone else watching sees a woman reach into empty space and violate the structure of reality. That gap between self-perception and reputation is free characterization.

### 17.6 Off-Page Development, Concretely (extends the already-locked "world doesn't wait" principle)

🟢 Off-page development should change **circumstances** more than **core character direction** — a returning character should be recognizable, but the reader should immediately register *I missed part of their life*. Concrete examples worth reusing directly: a guild contractor returns having founded a small guild of their own; a Bailian protagonist has acquired followers they never wanted; an Erkhaan officer has been promoted and hates what the promotion required; a Harrowed survivor has become Named; Kasane finds someone she remembers as powerless now commanding a room. **Leave evidence, don't summarize:** changed clothing, new scars, different subordinates, altered speech patterns, missing equipment, changed rank markers, someone using a new title or refusing an old one — let Kasane notice first and let explanation arrive later.

### 17.7 Mid-Saga Ensemble Mechanics

🟢 Relationships should form **pairwise, not universally** — Kasane trusts A, A distrusts B, B owes C, C respects Kasane but dislikes A, D has never met A but heard something false about them. That's an actual social graph, not five people who all independently like the protagonist. **Found family is earned, never a starting trait** — the group begins from overlapping necessity, mutual usefulness, shared enemies, or debt, and affection follows a progression nobody announces: stranger → known quantity → useful → reliable → trusted → missed when absent → someone whose loss changes your decisions. Members should not all like each other equally — some pairs read as sibling-like, some purely professional, some competitive, some deeply loyal but personally irritating; two members can trust each other with their lives without ever becoming close, which is preferable to emotional homogeneity. **Regional habits survive convergence** — a Yamashiro character still thinks in territory and obligation, a Haejin character asks who's paying, a Bailian character interprets events spiritually, an Erkhaan character immediately sets watches and supply requirements, a Harrowed character distrusts any plan dependent on institutional rescue; convergence makes origin interact, it doesn't erase it. **Terminology bleeds as a marker of intimacy** — a character using another's regional Gate-word (Rent/Aperture/Revelation/Incursion Point/Hole) without noticing can say more than an explicit declaration of closeness.

### 17.8 Power Balance, Rescue Reciprocity, and Substantive Disagreement

🟢 Don't artificially weaken Kasane to make room for others — give them problems she genuinely cannot solve (military command, Ura scholarship, healing, negotiation, information networks, conventional combat, logistics, political legitimacy). **Rescue reciprocity:** every major ensemble member should save Kasane at least once and need her at least once — saving doesn't have to be physical; it can be political intervention, correcting a fatal assumption, protecting her reputation, or telling her something she doesn't want to hear. **Disagreements need real stakes:** close a Gate or exploit it, reveal information or suppress panic, obey command or desert, protect one person or a settlement, trust an Ura intelligence, use the Stitch despite unknown cost — sometimes Kasane is wrong, sometimes the person opposing her is wrong, sometimes both choices cause damage. Trust should survive disagreement only after actually being tested by it. **The ensemble should fracture seriously at least once**, from incompatible principles rather than a cheap misunderstanding a single sentence could resolve — readers should understand both sides, and reunion (if it happens) should require changed circumstances or changed people, not just an apology.

### 17.9 Kasane's Leadership Arc, and Contextual Leadership

🟢 Kasane shouldn't want leadership — her instinct is *tell me the problem and let me work on it*. Leadership forces the harder recognition that **people are not kilns**; they can't simply be optimized. Others may eventually look to her because she sees connections, stays functional under pressure, crosses boundaries, keeps promises, and doesn't demand reverence — but she becomes *capable of* leadership without needing to become the permanent commander. Leadership should stay contextual and visibly redistribute competence: someone else commands in battle, another leads negotiation, Kasane may lead during Ura transit, the Bailian protagonist takes point on religious disputes, the Erkhaan protagonist on military logistics, a local Harrowed survivor on Harrowed territory — nobody becomes party leader just because the genre expects one.

### 17.10 The Erosion as Convergence Pressure, and the Five Answers as Late-Saga Tools (extends Section 14.1 and Section 11.14's endgame question)

🟢 The Erosion doesn't deliberately assemble the cast — its expanding effects simply stop letting regional problems stay regional, and each protagonist ends up holding a different piece of the same picture because their home civilization encountered a different facet of it. The ensemble becomes necessary not because it accumulates enough firepower, but because it accumulates enough **perspectives**. By late saga, each region's original "answer" (Section 14.1) becomes a genuine late-game tool rather than just a cultural label: Yamashiro's containment teaches that boundaries can protect; Haejin's utilization teaches that danger can be worked with rather than only feared; Bailian's faith teaches that meaning changes behavior and an incomplete model can still preserve real truth; Erkhaan's war teaches that some threats genuinely must be resisted at enormous cost; the Harrowed's survival teaches that people continue when institutions and assumptions fail. The endgame needs all five — none alone is sufficient, which is the character-level version of Section 11.14's "no single worldview contains enough of the truth."

### 17.11 Managing Late-Saga Scale

🟢 Abandon "one region owns one arc" by late saga — conflicts can run simultaneously across multiple regions plus the Ura itself, POV rotates by stakes rather than rotation order, and the ensemble can split into separate groups so not everyone needs to be present at every climax. **Four character tiers keep the cast from bloating:** Core Ensemble (the emotional spine), Major Recurring Characters (Clan leaders, guild figures, clergy, officers, Named survivors — real arcs, not equal page time), Regional Cast (tied to one place, keep existing after the protagonists leave), and Historical/Mythic Figures (encountered only through records, legends, ruins, or inherited consequences). Not every interesting character needs to join the traveling cast. **Civilizations beyond the five core regions** (Qamar, Vesper, Meridian, the Frost Kingdoms, the Seven Archipelagos, Section 13.7) don't each need their own full "new protagonist joins the party" cycle — some may introduce a major recurring character, others may only expand geopolitics, Ura knowledge, or competing solutions to the Erosion.

**Death and departure without automatic punishment:** found family doesn't grant plot immunity, but death shouldn't be the default way to prove stakes are real. Equally valid permanent consequences: disability, lost power, memory loss, exile, changed allegiance, retirement, responsibility that prevents further travel, broken trust, political office, or simply choosing home over the ensemble. A character can leave the active ensemble without being punished for it — sometimes staying somewhere *is* the character's growth.

### 17.12 Power Progression, the System's Limits, and the Final Conflict

🟢 Characters shouldn't scale identically — improvement can come through combat mastery, ability evolution, institutional authority, information, political influence, equipment, experience, or relationships, and a protagonist can return having gained no combat rank at all while being far more consequential than before; this also protects the saga from endless numerical escalation. **The System records consequences, it doesn't supply meaning** — a protagonist's emotional climax should almost never just be "I reached A-rank"; rank advancement can accompany a real transformation (choice, belief, responsibility, relationship, identity) but should never substitute for one.

**The final conflict is not Kasane's solo fight.** Her interaction with the seam makes her mechanically central, but resolution should require things only other protagonists can do — holding political cooperation together, interpreting necessary information, commanding a front, preserving civilian survival, preventing exploitation of the crisis — while Kasane performs the one boundary-interaction nobody else can. Standing rule: **Kasane can do something nobody else can; Kasane cannot do everything that must be done.** And per Section 11.14's endgame question, she should end up with extraordinary power over the decision affecting both worlds without ending up with unilateral moral ownership of it — her growth is demonstrated by refusing to repeat, at larger scale, the very mistake the saga opened on: institutions deciding what she is without asking her.

### 17.13 Saga Rhythm, and Remembering Where It Started

🟢 The overall saga movement, as a checkable eight-beat rhythm: **Discovery** (Kasane and Yamashiro) → **Contrast** (other civilizations reveal Yamashiro's assumptions were regional, not universal) → **Pattern** (separate anomalies start resembling each other) → **Intersection** (characters and consequences cross regional boundaries) → **Convergence** (the Erosion becomes impossible to treat as separate local problems) → **Fracture** (different answers to the crisis strain alliances, Section 17.8) → **Understanding** (the cast learns both the Ura and the boundary are more complicated than any single regional model predicted) → **Choice** (the question becomes what form of coexistence between realities can survive, not simply whether humanity can preserve its world — Section 11.14).

However large the world gets, the saga should periodically return to mundane measures: Kasane should still notice smoke, bad tools, wet wood, hunger, damaged roofs, whether someone's eaten, whether a fire is breathing correctly (extends Section 18's habits material). Cosmic stakes should never fully replace human-scale ones — the reason the worlds matter, at the end, is that people live in them.

### 17.14 Writing Rules — Convergent Ensemble (extends Section 1's general craft rulebook)

**Always:** complete each protagonist before convergence; give every protagonist relationships independent of Kasane; let characters change off-page with evidence, not exposition; preserve regional habits after convergence; let protagonists meet without Kasane present; keep leadership contextual; distribute competence; let Kasane be wrong sometimes; let others save her and let her save others; make disagreements substantive; build relationships pairwise; let characters leave without punishment; keep local stakes alive during global escalation; let the Erosion connect plots through consequence, never coincidence.

**Never:** reduce later protagonists to party roles; repeat the Kasane-Parade pattern; freeze characters between appearances; make everyone instantly trust her; make everyone equally close to each other; erase cultural differences after convergence; make her automatically command the group; let Null Passage solve every climax; force every major character into the traveling cast; resolve an ensemble fracture with a cheap misunderstanding; let rank advancement substitute for character development; rush found-family banter before it's earned; make the final conflict a Kasane solo victory.

**One-line summary:** *Five people begin by learning how to survive the systems that defined them; they end by discovering that no single person, culture, or world possesses enough of the truth to survive alone.* **Structural north star:** the saga starts with Kasane because someone has to be first, and stops belonging only to her because that's the point — she remains first among equals because the Stitch makes her the thread capable of crossing the seams between everyone else's stories, but a stitch is not the whole cloth.

## SECTION 17 ADDENDUM — PROPOSED ARC SEEDS (❓ ALL DEVELOPMENT QUESTIONS — NONE OF THIS IS LOCKED)

### Proposed Arc Order

Yamashiro (Arc 1, in progress) → **Haejin** → **Bailian** → **Erkhaan** → **the Harrowed**, then convergence.

Reasoning: this runs the moral register from grayer (Haejin) through faith-vs-power (Bailian) to darkest (Erkhaan) before landing on the Harrowed — which structurally needs to come after at least one or two other arcs have produced the "refuse" it's built to receive (Section 13.4). It also lets Erkhaan's Long Vigil, the highest-stakes front, be the arc that makes the Erosion's escalation undeniable right before convergence begins. This is a recommendation, not a requirement — nothing prevents reordering later.

### Haejin — Arc 2 Proposal

- **Protagonist (primary option):** A contracted extraction-guild Hunter, sponsored young by a Deep Ports upstart guild, now working to buy out or break a predatory sponsorship contract (Section 13.1). Personal stake: literal legal freedom, not survival — a deliberate tonal contrast to Kasane's arc.
  - *Alternate A:* A financier-guild heir who discovers what the contract system actually costs the people beneath them and turns reformer against their own class.
  - *Alternate B:* A Salt Islands smuggler-captain operating just outside guild oversight — lighter, heist-flavored register.
- **Antagonist:** Not a single villain — the financier-guild structure itself, personified through one financier who sincerely believes the system is necessary and stable (consistent with Section 13.1's "every guild benefits from the system as-is"), plus a rival guild master who exploits the protagonist's contract for advantage in the Old Pearl Coast/Deep Ports rivalry.
- **Myth fragment surfaced:** The Tideless Mile (Section 8/13.1) — reframed here as a labor/safety issue guild leadership would rather not investigate than as pure superstition.
- **Off-page world change carried in from Arc 1:** The Old Pearl Coast/Deep Ports rivalry has sharpened; at least one background Gate surge (Section 11) has shifted which guild type — extraction or logistics — currently holds the upper hand.

### Bailian — Arc 3 Proposal

- **Protagonist:** A reformist Thousand Monasteries scholar-monk pursuing the suppressed Hollow Monk account (Section 13.2), whose investigation forces a personal reckoning with doctrine rather than just an institutional one — do they still believe, if the Communion's founding claim is wrong?
  - *Alternate:* A Jade Frontier military-religious governor whose pragmatic, combat-focused faith puts them at odds with central doctrine during a crisis — a harder, less contemplative register than the scholar-monk option.
- **Antagonist:** A senior orthodox cleric who genuinely believes doctrine protects people (Section 13.2's "both motives sincerely held") — the obstacle is conviction, not villainy, consistent with the "factions are non-binary" craft rule.
- **Myth fragment surfaced:** The Hollow Monk directly; possible glancing reference to the Forbidden Provinces' restricted evidence (Section 13.2) without opening it fully.
- **Off-page world change carried in:** The orthodox/reformist clerical rift (Section 13.2) has widened since Arc 1, likely accelerated by whatever happened in Haejin reaching Bailian's ports in degraded form (Section 16's cross-regional rumor degradation).

### Erkhaan — Arc 4 Proposal

- **Protagonist:** A newly conscripted Northern Herdlands youth (Section 13.3's suggested emotional entry point) — structurally mirrors Kasane's "newly Marked" opening, but conscription replaces accident: no ambiguity about how they entered the system, only about what it costs them.
  - *Alternate/companion figure:* A veteran or deserter carrying a suppressed field report (Section 5's Classification Model blind spot) — could run as a mentor figure to the conscript protagonist, or as this arc's protagonist instead if a more jaded, mid-career entry point is preferred.
- **Antagonist:** A competent, non-sadistic commanding officer who genuinely believes conscription is the only thing holding the Long Vigil — consistent with the locked line that "the argument for the system is not absurd." Tragedy, not melodrama.
- **Myth fragment surfaced:** The Hundred-Year Legion (Section 9/13.3) — the roster-number superstition becomes personally relevant if the protagonist's unit inherits one of those numbers.
- **Off-page world change carried in:** The Long Vigil has intensified — a background Erosion surge (Section 11) makes this the arc where escalation becomes impossible for any single region to read as a local problem.

### The Harrowed — Arc 5 Proposal

- **Protagonist:** Deliberately someone whose backstory ties to a region *other* than Yamashiro or Haejin/Bailian/Erkhaan specifically to maximize convergence value — strongest option is the Erkhaan deserter thread from Arc 4 arriving here as a Named or newly-arriving Unknown, directly using the "world's refuse washes up in the Harrowed" mechanism (Section 13.4) to link two arcs without contrivance.
  - *Alternate:* A Bailian-declared heretic connected to the Hollow Monk thread, arriving after Arc 3, giving the Harrowed a second independent convergence hook instead of relying solely on Erkhaan.
- **Antagonist:** No institution to personify — the antagonist function here is closer to a specific Deep Harrow threat (Section 13.4/10's dormant/ancient entities and unmirrored ground) or a fragile Last Light political crisis threatening the neutral ground's mutual-restraint equilibrium.
- **Myth fragment surfaced:** None invented — per Section 13.4, the Harrowed's accounts are the least-filtered version of *all* the previous fragments arriving secondhand, making this arc the place where the reader (not any character) can start to see the fragments as one picture.
- **Off-page world change carried in:** By this point, all four prior regions have had at least one background surge and one internal shift — this arc is where their accumulated consequences physically arrive as people, not just as rumor.

**Continuity note:** None of the above touches Chapter 1 or contradicts anything locked in Sections 5–17. Treat every bullet above as a draggable proposal, not a commitment — confirm, mix-and-match, or discard per region independently.
