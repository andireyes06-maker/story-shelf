## Approved Foundation

### Region and time

- 🟢 Arc 2 takes place in **Haejin**, centered on the Deep Ports and their conflict with Old Pearl Coast capital.
- 🟢 The arc opens **eight months after Arc 1**.
- 🟢 Kasane and the Yamashiro cast have changed during the gap. Kasane has completed sustained supervised training with Isato and has gained field experience. The Five Limits and the Stitch's physical cost still hold.

### Thematic question

> What remains yours when everything valuable about you can be assigned a price?

### Primary protagonist

- 🟢 **Kang Seorin**, female, age thirty.
- 🟢 Deep Ports native, born to a dockworking family.
- 🟢 Marked at about fourteen during an industrial sea-Aperture accident.
- 🟢 Her first manifestation saved lives and caused structural damage that left her family exposed to claims they could not pay.
- 🟢 A small Deep Ports extraction guild sponsored her, settled the claims, trained her, and built much of its later success around her output.
- 🟢 Current rank: **A-rank, Sovereign Arm**.
- 🟢 Public position: celebrated extraction Hunter and market-moving guild asset.
- 🟢 Legal position: her sponsoring guild owns her Hunter license and the rights attached to her work.

### Seorin's personal want

Seorin wants to purchase legal ownership of her license before her sponsor renews, extends, or sells the contract. She plans to establish a small independent extraction operation whose jobs she can choose.

She has saved the stated buyout price before the arc opens. When she invokes the clause, she learns that her guild has pledged her future yield against fleet expansion and its annual Compact position. The underwriter orders a new valuation based on replacement cost, recovery expenses, and lost future earnings. The guild prepares to extend or transfer the claim.

### Need and flaw

Seorin begins with a narrow definition of freedom: nobody else can impose a limit on her once she owns her license. Sixteen years of sponsorship taught her that each relationship reveals a price, so she tests loyalty by making herself difficult to remain loyal to.

She needs to distinguish chosen obligation from ownership. Her restraint must belong to her. Freedom gives her authority over her choices and leaves her answerable for the damage those choices cause.

### Distinctive power

🟡 **Working mechanical description.** The System records the ability as `[ABILITY: SUBSTRATE SEVERANCE]`. Munyeong markets it as **Perfect Draw**. Seorin calls it **the pull**.

Seorin perceives and severs the bond between nonliving Ura-derived matter and the material that contains or supports it.

In extraction work, she can recover intact Ura veins, cores, deposits, and embedded fragments from stone, wreckage, seabed, or collapsed Aperture structures. Her precision turns work that would consume weeks and crews into a short operation.

Used against Haejin infrastructure, the same ability can remove Ura-treated components from ship hulls, cranes, seawalls, harbor pilings, foundations, weapons, vaults, and extraction machinery. Haejin's use of Ura material makes its richest ports vulnerable to her.

#### Boundaries and cost

- Her ability affects nonliving Ura-derived or Ura-saturated matter.
- She must understand the target material and its place in the surrounding structure.
- Touch gives her the greatest precision. Connected structures permit broader propagation with less control.
- She cannot safely extract unknown material from a living body.
- She does not control the collapse that follows removal of a load-bearing element.
- Her body absorbs part of the released structural strain. Heavy use causes joint damage, fractures, internal bleeding, and confusion between a target's stress and her own pain.
- Large-scale use can continue past safe limits, but she remains inside the destruction she initiates.

### Antagonist pressure

The primary human antagonist is the senior financier-guild underwriter who approved Seorin's sponsorship when established interests considered her too dangerous.

The underwriter believes Haejin's sponsorship system spreads the cost of training, equipment, legal standing, injury, failed candidates, and death. Successful Hunters must repay more than their personal expenses because their returns fund the candidates who fail. If valuable Hunters can leave when they become profitable, financiers will stop backing poor and unproven Marked children.

The underwriter takes pride in Seorin's career and regards the original sponsorship as a rescue. They offer status, wealth, authority, and safer terms in place of self-ownership. Seorin's eventual rampage supplies evidence for their belief that an external restraint protected the public.

A rival guild master may exploit the dispute or seek to acquire Seorin's contract. That figure remains a secondary opportunist rather than the arc's moral antagonist.

### Kasane's entry

A Deep Ports extraction guild sends Yamashiro a formal request after finding a profitable sea-Aperture whose boundary instability prevents conventional extraction. Haejin believes Kasane's public stabilization technique may hold the site long enough to recover its resources.

Kuromori approves the request as supervised field development. Isato travels with Kasane as her handler. Shirogane retains review authority and places limits on the work. Kasane accepts because the site may teach her something that controlled exercises cannot.

The request also functions as a commercial assessment. Haejin guilds want to price Kasane's ability, determine whether it can become a contracted service, and decide who could claim the value she creates. Kasane does not understand the full commercial purpose when she arrives.

Seorin serves as the local extraction lead. Her performance affects her contract valuation, and the site requires both her material knowledge and Kasane's boundary perception.

The first unstable Aperture remains separate from the Tideless Mile. Evidence may point toward the Mile later, but Kasane does not receive immediate access to Haejin's protected mystery.

### POV ownership

- Seorin owns about two thirds of the arc's scenes.
- Kasane owns most of the remaining scenes.
- A small number of local supporting viewpoints may show consequences neither protagonist can witness.
- Each scene keeps one POV.
- The arc opens with Seorin and establishes her work, contract, relationships, and buyout attempt before Kasane enters Haejin.
- Seorin owns the rampage decision, moral reckoning, climax choice, and ending.

### Arc 1 carryovers

## Arc Movement

### Opening state

Seorin leads profitable extraction work for the Deep Ports guild that sponsored her. Guild workers, contractors, rivals, and financiers plan around her availability. Her family lives better because of the contract. Her sponsor's fleet and Compact position depend on her projected yield.

She presents the money required by her buyout clause. The underwriter rejects the old valuation and reveals that the guild has already pledged her future work. The next annual reassessment will extend or transfer the claim. Seorin's private plan now has a deadline.

### Movement I: The Price That Moved

**Job:** Establish ordinary Haejin life, Seorin's exceptional status, the guild's dependence on her, and the relationships that existed before Kasane.

**Causal turn:** Seorin invokes the buyout clause. The underwriter revalues her contract and exposes the obligations secured against her future yield.

**Required consequence:** Seorin can no longer earn freedom by following the existing agreement. She begins seeking leverage outside the sponsor's preferred process.

### Movement II: A Foreign Asset

**Job:** Bring Kasane into a story already in motion.

Kasane and Isato arrive under the joint-oversight arrangement. Haejin representatives treat her as an unpriced capability. Seorin recognizes the assessment because institutions assessed her the same way at fourteen.

Kasane appears stronger than she was in Arc 1. She can perform precise, deliberate Stitch work within the established limits. Seorin remains the indispensable authority on the sea, extraction, and Ura-built infrastructure.

**Causal turn:** Initial assessment shows that the Aperture can produce valuable material, but its instability exceeds the conditions described in the request.

**Required consequence:** Cancelling the operation would damage the sponsor's finances and weaken its Compact position. Leadership demands more testing while controlling who receives the safety findings.

### Movement III: Terms of Use

**Job:** Force Seorin and Kasane into working proximity without turning the arc into recruitment.

Seorin uses the operation to seek financial or legal leverage for her buyout. The underwriter offers improved terms and authority inside the guild. Kasane's oversight clauses collide with Haejin's assumptions about operational ownership and salvage rights.

Seorin and Kasane disagree about restraint. Kasane sees missing information as a reason to stop. Seorin has spent sixteen years watching institutions use uncertainty to delay freedom while accepting uncertainty when profit is available.

**Causal turn:** They find evidence that leadership concealed earlier signs of structural danger at the site.

**Required consequence:** Seorin refuses the next extraction order. The guild prepares to enforce the contract and protect the site's valuation.

### Movement IV: Replacement Cost

**Job:** Demonstrate how the system responds when its most valuable asset refuses.

The guild cannot compel Seorin by physical force. It uses assignments, crew funding, family exposure, license status, and the younger sponsored Hunter's ambitions. Leadership sends a less experienced team into the site without Seorin after minimizing the warning.

Kasane cannot authorize Haejin's operation or invalidate Seorin's contract. She can document the boundary behavior and refuse to certify safety.

**Causal turn:** The operation fails. Hunters and laborers become trapped inside a collapsing Ura-reinforced harbor sector. Leadership delays destructive rescue measures to preserve the site, fleet collateral, and salvage claims.

**Required consequence:** Seorin decides that legal process has priced the trapped crews below the infrastructure around them.

### Movement V: What Held the Harbor Together

**Job:** Deliver the approved rampage as an irreversible character choice with visible regional cost.

The rampage has three stages:

1. **Rescue.** Seorin dismantles the harbor sector to reach the trapped crews. The destruction is necessary and saves lives.
2. **Retaliation.** Once rescue access exists, she turns her ability against the sponsor's fleet, machinery, fortified offices, and Ura-built assets.
3. **Recognition.** Seorin understands that necessity has ended and chooses how far to continue.

Kasane evacuates civilians and stabilizes isolated failures where the Five Limits permit. She cannot stop Seorin without repeating the claim that someone else has the right to govern Seorin's power.

**Required consequence:** Seorin saves people and destroys part of the Deep Ports economy. The underwriter gains proof for the claim that contracts protect Haejin from powerful Hunters. Seorin incurs physical injury and public liability.

### Movement VI: The Account After

**Job:** Keep destruction on the page after the spectacle ends.

Shipping routes change. Workers lose income. Salvage crews search wreckage. Insurance and contract prices rise. Rivals purchase damaged assets. Residents circulate incompatible accounts of Seorin's actions. Some contracted Hunters treat her as proof that resistance is possible; others blame her for tightening guild control.

Seorin cannot describe the entire rampage as rescue. Kasane can testify about the immediate danger and leadership's concealment, but she cannot absolve Seorin's retaliation.

**Causal turn:** Seorin acquires evidence that the underwriter and sponsor manipulated safety findings, extraction decisions, or contract valuation.

**Required consequence:** The underwriter offers Seorin the settlement she wanted before the arc began.

### Movement VII: Ownership of the Damage

**Job:** Give Seorin a decision Kasane cannot make.

The underwriter offers legal ownership of Seorin's license, discharge of the remaining debt, and enough operating rights to begin an independent concern. In exchange, Seorin must surrender the evidence and accept a contained official account of the rampage. The system and the other sponsored contracts remain intact.

Seorin rejects the private settlement. She accepts public responsibility for the destruction, prevents the guild from blaming her crew, and exposes the concealed risk and coercive valuation practices. Her choice places her license, wealth, and future at risk.

Kasane can establish technical facts. She cannot decide how Seorin weighs her crew, her guilt, or her freedom.

**Required consequence:** The Compact must hear a public contract challenge from a Hunter strong enough to survive its retaliation.

### Movement VIII: Chosen Terms

**Job:** Complete Seorin's movement and leave Haejin changed without fixing it.

The Compact creates a narrow severance precedent: a sponsor may lose its ownership claim when it conceals operational risk or manipulates a Hunter's valuation through unsafe assignments. The Compact frames the ruling as punishment for documented misconduct rather than recognition that sponsored Hunters own themselves.

Seorin gains legal ownership of her license. Liability, restitution, lost equipment, and rebuilding costs consume much of what she saved. She begins the independent operation with fewer resources than planned. Some crew members join her; others refuse or accept security under another guild.

The sponsor fractures or loses standing. The underwriter loses control of Seorin's account and may lose formal authority, but financier guilds rewrite future contracts to close the exposed weakness. Most copycat petitions fail. Rival guilds profit from distressed assets. The damaged harbor remains visible.

Seorin's operation adopts chosen terms: crew refusal rights, disclosure of risk findings, and pre-deployment agreement on salvage shares and liability. These terms do not repair Haejin. They create one uncertain alternative inside it.

The arc closes with Seorin's first independent operation leaving through a harbor that still bears the damage she caused.

## Character Movement

### Seorin

**Beginning:** Seorin believes ownership of her license will remove other people's right to restrain her.

**Pressure:** The guild reveals that it has spent her future before she could purchase it. She sees Kasane enter Haejin as another valuable person undergoing appraisal.

**Break:** Seorin uses her full capability to save trapped workers, then continues into retaliation. She proves that nobody present can stop her and learns that unopposed action still creates obligations.

**Climax:** She rejects a private freedom purchased through silence, owns the damage, and protects the crew from scapegoating.

**End:** Seorin chooses the obligations of her new operation and accepts their cost. She remains angry, dangerous, and capable of catastrophic force. Her restraint now rests on decisions she claims as her own.

### Kasane

Kasane enters as a stronger field observer under contested supervision. Haejin prices her ability before it understands her. Seorin shows her what a life can become when an institution builds its prosperity around one person's exceptional power.

Kasane's arc remains secondary. She must decide when technical cooperation becomes complicity, act within the Five Limits during the harbor disaster, and resist both Haejin ownership claims and the temptation to substitute her judgment for Seorin's. She leaves with new evidence about sea-Aperture behavior and sharper awareness that institutions can convert protection into a permanent claim.

## Institutional Pressure Map

### Deep Ports sponsor

Needs Seorin's output to service fleet obligations and preserve Compact influence. Its leaders may care about her while treating release as institutional suicide.

### Financier guild

Treats Seorin as proof that sponsorship works and as collateral supporting wider risk. It fears a precedent that allows profitable Hunters to leave after financiers absorb early losses.

### Old Pearl Coast interests

Can acquire the contract, distressed Deep Ports assets, or both. They prefer a weakened upstart and a controllable Seorin.

### Compact

Protects contract confidence, Gate revenue, and port function. It will concede a narrow misconduct ruling before it recognizes a broad right to self-ownership.

### Workers and sponsored Hunters

Depend on guild wages, legal standing, and equipment. They bear the first economic cost of Seorin's revolt and hold conflicting views of what she did.

### Yamashiro oversight

Kuromori wants field knowledge and a defensible account of Kasane's conduct. Shirogane wants strict limits and retains grounds to challenge the trip. Neither authority controls Haejin's contract law.

## Required Tests Before Chapter Outlining

### Independence test

Remove Kasane. Seorin still invokes the buyout clause, confronts the revaluation, refuses unsafe work, responds to the failed replacement operation, causes the rampage, faces liability, and chooses between private freedom and disclosure. Kasane changes the evidence, rescue options, and relationship pressure. She does not create Seorin's story.

### Anti-Kasane-Parade test

Kasane arrives because Haejin requested a bounded technical service. She cannot solve contract ownership, command the extraction crews, prevent Seorin's chosen retaliation, decide the public settlement, or build Seorin's independent operation.

### Protagonist distinction test

Seorin's power, public status, commercial education, crew command, anger, and infrastructure knowledge come from Haejin. Moving her unchanged to Yamashiro, Bailian, Erkhaan, or the Harrowed would break her legal position, methods, relationships, and central conflict.

### Arc-ending ceiling

The ruling frees Seorin through a narrow misconduct finding. Haejin retains sponsorship, guild ownership, annual wealth-weighted Compact power, risk incentives, and financier influence. Other guilds adapt faster than most Hunters can use the precedent.
