## MARKING — MECHANICS AND PERCEPTION

## SECTION 7 — PERCEPTION AND MARKING

### A. Canon Foundation

🔒 One eye changes when a person is Marked — iris pattern shifts, goes faintly luminous under stress or ability use. Kasane's exception: both eyes shift, immediately visible as anomalous.

### B. Deep Expansion

**The Marking event** 🟢
Marking occurs at proximity to unusually high Ura activity — overwhelmingly, this means a Gate, though the handoff already establishes Kasane's Marking as an atypical case (an Erosion working, not a standard Gate). A Marking event is not guaranteed by proximity: most people near a Gate are never Marked. 🟡 Working hypothesis (widely held, unproven): Marking selects people who are already, in some unmeasurable way, "close to the seam" — a vague and institutionally convenient explanation, since it lets every region attach its own theology or genetics to it without contradicting observed data.

**Failure states** 🟢
Not every Marking event resolves cleanly. Documented outcomes beyond standard single-eye Marking:
- **Null Marking** — proximity and exposure occur, but no Marking results. The overwhelming majority outcome, hence why Gates don't depopulate everything nearby.
- **Partial Marking** — perception without full interaction capability, or vice versa. Rare, poorly understood, often mistaken for standard Marking until it fails a Marked person in a crisis.
- **Kasane's anomaly (dual-eye)** — the only confirmed case of its kind. Whether other anomalous Markings exist elsewhere in the world is 🟡 an open institutional question — no region has records of another case, but recordkeeping quality varies enormously (Erkhaan's Classification Model, Section 5, would likely misfile or discard such a case rather than preserve it).
- **Fatal Marking attempts** — a minority of Marking-adjacent exposures kill rather than Mark. This is part of why proximity to a live Gate is treated as lethal-risk-plus-opportunity rather than a lottery worth chasing, which matters for Section 14's institutional response.

**Reversibility, transfer, inheritance, inducement** 🔵/🟡
- *Reversal:* No confirmed case of a true Marking reversal exists anywhere. 🔵 Widely believed impossible; 🟡 not rigorously proven impossible, just never observed.
- *Transfer:* Not observed. Multiple regions have folklore about it (usually framed as a curse or a bargain), which should be read as myth, not mechanism, unless a later arc deliberately introduces a real case.
- *Inheritance:* Marking itself does not pass genetically — a Marked parent's child is not more likely to be Marked at birth. However, 🟡 several regions (most institutionally, Yamashiro's Clans) believe otherwise, because Marking *does* cluster in certain bloodlines at a rate too high for coincidence. The actual mechanism (proximity to hereditary Gate-territory stewardship, not blood) is 🟢 the likelier explanation, but it's politically inconvenient for the Clans to accept, since hereditary "blessing" justifies their power (see handoff, Section 6).
- *Inducement:* Not achievable through any confirmed method. This is a closely guarded null result — several institutions have quietly tried and failed, and would very much prefer their failure not become common knowledge, since public despair or public relief both destabilize a hierarchy built partly on the idea that Marking is special.
- *Faking:* The visible sign (iris shift, luminosity under stress) is not reproducible by unmarked people through any known cosmetic or medical means — a genuine Marked person can always be distinguished from an impostor by someone who knows what to look for, though "knowing what to look for" is itself unevenly distributed (a border guard versus a village elder versus a corrupt clan retainer will each catch different kinds of fraud).

**Animals and indirect interaction** 🟢
Some animals show behavioral response to Ura proximity (skittishness, refusal to cross certain ground) without themselves being Marked or perceiving the Ura the way a Marked human does — this is the mechanism behind the Chapter 1 setup's "the town already knows something happened — the animals" beat, and should be treated as a real, consistent world rule rather than a one-off spooky detail.

### C. Consequences
- The Clans' hereditary-blessing narrative and the more mundane "proximity to stewardship" explanation can coexist in-world without either being narrated as flatly correct — this is useful tension rather than something to resolve.
- Fatal Marking risk means Gate zones are not treated as opportunity sites by desperate populations the way one might expect from pure game logic — the real risk of death suppresses that behavior, which matters for Section 14 (how ordinary people actually relate to Gates).

### D. Institutions
- Every region needs some formal or informal apparatus for confirming genuine Marking (given fraud is a live if difficult possibility) — this should be developed concretely per-region in Sections 12–13 rather than assumed identical everywhere.
- Suppressed null-results (failed inducement attempts, failed reversal attempts) are a strong candidate for a recurring "buried research" narrative device across multiple regions.

### E. Ordinary Life
- Folk belief about Marking (curses, transfer, inheritance) should circulate as genuine oral tradition — vivid, specific, regionally flavored — without the narration ever needing to confirm or debunk it directly; let context and the rumor-evaluation lens (handoff Section 16 topic) do that work.

### F. Internal Contradictions
- The Clans' bloodline theology is quietly undermined by their own stewardship-proximity data, if anyone ever cross-referenced it properly — a slow-burn internal contradiction some Clan scholar-character could stumble into.

### G. Narrative Opportunities
- A secondary character whose family has tried and failed to induce Marking in a desperate relative — personal, costly, and never publicly discussed.
- A false Marked person operating on forged credentility in a region with weaker verification norms (a strong Harrowed or Haejin-flavored plot seed).

### H. Unresolved Questions (Open Mysteries)
- ⚪ Whether Kasane's dual-eye anomaly has ever occurred before, anywhere, and gone unrecorded.
- ⚪ Whether reversal is truly impossible or simply never attempted by anyone who lived to report success.

### I. Continuity Notes
- Kasane's dual-eye status (handoff Section 5/6) is reinforced here as mechanically and historically unprecedented as far as any institution's records show — this should stay a live source of tension across every region she eventually interacts with, not just Yamashiro.
- The animal-response detail directly supports the already-planned Chapter 1 beat (handoff Section 12) — no changes needed there, just confirmation this is systemic, not incidental.

## THE STITCH / NULL PASSAGE — BASE MECHANICS (original locked handoff)

## 7. THE STITCH — KASANE'S POWER

Category: Unique / forbidden. Not rare-but-acceptable. The System has no category for it. Officially: Null Passage. Kasane calls it the Stitch.

**What It Does**
The membrane between the Ura and the living world is supposed to be one-way and Gate-controlled. Kasane can reach through it directly, anywhere, without a Gate. She can open the seam where there is no Gate. This should not be possible.

**Progression Arc**

```
+-------+----------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Phase | Capability                                               | Cost                                                     |
+-------+----------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Early | Involuntary glimpses through the seam during stress.     | Nosebleeds. Blackouts. Lost time. Psychological: she     |
|       | Brief "peeks" — she sees the Ura overlay for half a      | experiences things nobody else does, carries weight she  |
|       | second without choosing to. Momentary enhancement from   | can't share. Isolating.                                  |
|       | dipping an object into Ura-adjacent space (tools hit     |                                                          |
|       | harder, move stranger).                                  |                                                          |
+-------+----------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Mid   | Brief controlled transit — a step, a reach, throwing     | Still physically punishing. Sustained use causes more    |
|       | something through. Can choose when to use it, mostly.    | than nosebleeds — something is being spent.              |
|       | Still costly. Can now intentionally see the Ura overlay  |                                                          |
|       | at will.                                                 |                                                          |
+-------+----------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
| Late  | Sustained presence on both sides simultaneously.         | Real, permanent costs. Memory. A Trait. A relationship.  |
|       | Deliberately pulling Ura terrain or creatures through    | Something that does not come back.                       |
|       | the seam. Wielding the world's own weapon against        |                                                          |
|       | itself.                                                  |                                                          |
+-------+----------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Why It's Forbidden**
- The membrane is containment. Gates are controlled leaks. Someone who can open the seam anywhere at will is a walking uncontrolled breach — a mobile Gate with no timer and no location.
- Threatens both sides: Yamashiro's Hunter authorities see an unlicensed, uncontrollable liability. Whatever intelligence exists on the Ura's side may notice the seam being opened from the human side and take interest. Hostile or otherwise.
- Narrative engine: Kasane isn't malicious. Her mere existence is destabilizing. Other characters react to what the power is, not just who she is. Ongoing tension without requiring a cartoonish authority response.

**The Stitch and The Erosion**
Kasane's power works on the same membrane the Erosion is trying to tear. She could be the most effective weapon against it — she can seal the seam from within, not just fight what comes through. Or she could be exactly what the Erosion needs: someone who will open the seam for it, intentionally or not.

Keep this ambiguous. Do not resolve early.

**Cost Philosophy (General)**
Failure and growth cost something concrete and permanent. Memory, a Trait, a relationship, standing. Not instant death. Not free. Borrowed structurally from Shadow Slave — failure is common and expensive, not binary.

## THE STITCH / NULL PASSAGE — DEEP MECHANICS

## SECTION 8 — KASANE AND THE STITCH

### A. Canon Foundation

🔒 Kasane: 26, charcoal-burner, no clan surname, works the mid-mountain zone, dry pragmatist with craft-logic thinking, observational intelligence as her pre-Stitch edge, newly Marked with deliberately delayed processing. The Stitch/Null Passage: reaches through the membrane directly, without a Gate; progression from involuntary glimpses → controlled transit → sustained dual presence; costs escalate from nosebleeds/blackouts to permanent losses (memory, Trait, relationship, standing).

### B. Deep Expansion

**Physical mechanics** 🟢
Building on Section 6's time-perception rule: the Stitch does not "create" a passage so much as locally and briefly suspend the rule that human matter cannot interact with Ura matter absent a Gate. This is why it's officially classified as an anomaly rather than a skill — skills operate within the system's rules; the Stitch operates on them.

**Sensory signature** 🟢
When Kasane uses the Stitch, nearby Marked people (not unmarked ones) can sometimes perceive a brief, localized wrongness — a flicker at the edge of vision, a taste like scorched metal, ears popping as if from altitude change. This is a deliberate design choice: it means her ability is not perfectly secret from other Marked people even when she wants it to be, which sustains social/institutional tension rather than letting her hide indefinitely.

**Interaction with Gates** 🟡
Working hypothesis, untested in Arc 1: whether the Stitch can be used to influence, stabilize, or destabilize an existing Gate rather than opening independent access. This should remain unresolved until a scene actually requires testing it — a live capability question, not a a plot-convenience toggle.

**Interaction with the Erosion** ⚪
Established as an open mystery already (handoff Section 7, Section 9 of world bible) — the Erosion has "noticed" the seam being used from the human side. Deepening: this noticing is 🟢 confirmed to correlate with use frequency/intensity, not just existence — meaning heavier Stitch use is not narratively free even before permanent costs kick in; it plausibly draws more attention over time. This gives Kasane an in-fiction reason to ration use beyond her own body's cost, which enriches the tension without resolving what the Erosion's interest actually means.

**Failure modes** 🟢
- *Early-phase failure:* an involuntary glimpse triggers at a socially catastrophic moment (mid-conversation, mid-negotiation) rather than a moment of her choosing — this is a stronger recurring narrative device than pure combat failure, since it threatens her socially and politically, not just physically.
- *Mid-phase failure:* attempted controlled transit that partially fails — an object or limb briefly caught between states. Should be rare and treated as genuinely alarming, not a repeatable gag.
- *Late-phase failure:* Not yet reached in Arc 1; reserve full specification for when the story actually approaches that tier, per the "don't answer questions before they're asked" principle in Section 1's rulebook.

**Practical uses (early-phase, consistent with the progression table)** 🟢
- Dipping a tool into Ura-adjacent space briefly changes its practical properties (a blade "bites" stranger, a rope resists stranger) — should always come with an unpredictable, non-trivial side effect (the tool behaves oddly for a short time afterward, or she can't fully explain why it worked) so it never becomes a clean, repeatable trick.
- Involuntary half-second precognition-like glimpses — not full foresight, closer to noticing something is about to be wrong a beat before it visibly is. This should never let her cleanly dodge a real threat without cost; the warning is real but the reaction time/cost tradeoff should stay balanced.

### C. Consequences
- The sensory "leakage" to nearby Marked people means Kasane cannot use the Stitch in front of any Marked witness without risk, regardless of that witness's rank or allegiance — a built-in constraint that limits authorial temptation to let her use it freely whenever convenient.
- The Erosion's attention scaling with use frequency gives the story a soft, non-arbitrary throttle on how often the ability can appear without the plot needing to invent new reasons each time.

### D. Institutions
- Yamashiro's Hunter authorities (handoff Section 6) would, if they knew the full mechanics above, almost certainly attempt to restrict Kasane's *movement* rather than just her registration status — worth keeping in mind as her arc's political pressure develops.

### E. Ordinary Life
- Unmarked people near a Stitch use notice nothing directly (per the sensory-signature rule) — but might notice its secondary effects (a tool behaving oddly, an animal spooking) without understanding why, reinforcing Section 7's animal-response rule.

### F. Internal Contradictions
- The Erosion's scaling attention creates a tension between Kasane's practical need to use her only edge and the risk of drawing dangerous notice — this should read as a genuine dilemma, not a solved problem, throughout Arc 1.

### G. Narrative Opportunities
- A Marked ally or rival who "catches" the sensory wrongness of a Stitch use and has to decide whether to report it.
- A moment where the Stitch's early-phase involuntary glimpse fires during a politically sensitive Yamashiro negotiation, costing her socially rather than physically.

### H. Unresolved Questions (Open Mysteries)
- ⚪ Whether the Stitch can influence existing Gates — deliberately left untested.
- ⚪ What, precisely, the Erosion's "interest" in Stitch use actually means or wants.

### I. Continuity Notes
- The sensory-leakage mechanic should be treated as a hard constraint in Chapter 1 planning (handoff Section 12) if any other Marked character is present during her first uses.
- This section's cost mechanics explicitly build on Section 6's time-instability rule — keep that link intact in any future drafting; don't let the Stitch's costs drift into being unrelated to the general world physics.

### 8.1 What the Stitch Is Not (anti-power-creep boundary)

🟢 Explicitly not: teleportation, dimensional storage, ordinary phasing, portal creation, summoning, time travel, foresight, a pocket dimension, unrestricted wall-phasing, a second inventory, or a clean "enter the Ura" button. Several effects can superficially resemble these — the test is always whether the effect can be produced by changing how Kasane, an object, or a local area participates in the overlap between layers. If it can't, the Stitch shouldn't be able to do it. This is the standing check against scope creep for any future scene involving the ability.

### 8.2 Sensory Experience — Kasane's Own Perspective

🟢 Never a clean activation effect — the Stitch registers as physical misalignment: pressure behind both eyes, a metallic taste, one ear clearing before the other, fingertips briefly feeling too far from her hand, temperature arriving a fraction late, sound seeming to come through the wrong wall, her foot feeling planted somewhere her eyes disagree with. The core sensation is her body receiving contradictory information about which layer it occupies. She gets better at reading the contradiction with experience; she never gets fully comfortable with it.

### 8.3 The Five Limits (extends the cost/progression framework already locked)

🟢 Five independent constraints keep the Stitch from becoming unrestricted, and should be checked against any new use of the ability:
- **Contact:** requires an intuitive relationship between her and what she's moving — literal physical touch early, extendable to held objects and a small local area later. Never casual action at range ("point at something fifty meters off and move it") — distance sharply increases instability.
- **Mass:** more matter is harder, but mass isn't just physical weight — some Ura-adjacent things are "heavier to reality" than their size suggests, and she has no better term for this early on. Early: fingertips, a blade, a small object. Mid: a limb, another person's hand, her own body briefly. Late: another person, a large creature, sections of terrain.
- **Duration:** opening is easier than holding — a fraction of a second costs little, five seconds is dangerous, sustained overlap is exponentially harder because she's keeping two incompatible states from resolving against each other. This is where her charcoal-burning instinct becomes mechanically literal: she doesn't overpower the instability, she continuously adjusts it, the way she'd manage airflow through a burn that can blow out or flare if mishandled.
- **Precision:** a crude large effect isn't necessarily easier than a small precise one — moving an entire rock may be simpler than moving only a knife's cutting edge. Precision demands concentration, spatial awareness, and familiarity with both environments, giving her a technical progression independent of raw power.
- **Knowledge:** she can't safely Stitch into a place she understands nothing about — unseen Ura terrain risks solid matter already occupying the target space, hostile organisms, environmental hazards, or unknown boundary conditions. Early rule: *look before reaching.* Later rule: *understand before stepping.* This keeps information valuable even once her raw mobility improves.

### 8.4 Early-Phase Detail: Object-Dipping and Named Failure States

🟢 Object-dipping examples (non-repeatable, terrain/timing/angle-dependent — never a clean reusable trick, consistent with the existing "unpredictable side effect" rule): a hatchet edge biting through material wrong because it didn't interact with the full thickness normally; a rope partially anchoring against something invisible from the human side; a pole passing partly through an obstacle before snapping back to ordinary interaction.

Named early failure states, useful as a reusable taxonomy rather than one-off mishaps: **Slip** (unintentional activation), **Misalignment** (reaching for one layer, interacting with the other), **Rebound** (the seam rejects the attempted movement and snaps the object back), **Carryover** (something stays Ura-interactive longer than intended), **Missing Beat** (she loses a fragment of experienced time — someone finishes a sentence she never heard, a cup has moved), and **Witnessed Use** — the ability works correctly and the failure is that someone important saw it, which should be one of the most common early "failures," not a rare one.

### 8.5 Mid-Phase Signature Technique: The Step, and Combat Applications

🟢 **The Step** — identify a viable overlap, push partially into Ura participation, move, return to full human-world interaction. To an observer this reads as a skip, flicker, or impossible sidestep; Kasane actually crosses the intervening Ura terrain, so if the corresponding space is obstructed, hostile, or simply absent, the Step becomes dangerous or impossible. Restates and sharpens an existing point: **the Stitch does not eliminate geography — it gives her a second geography to navigate.**

Combat applications, all positional rather than a shift toward energy-blast fighting: **Offset Strike** (part of a weapon interacts from an unexpected angle via a brief Ura crossing), **False Cover** (using an obstacle that only exists on one side), **Disengagement** (a brief Step breaks an opponent's expected line of movement), **Boundary Trip** (a small environmental object partially displaced to interfere with pursuit), **Retrieval** (reaching through to recover an otherwise inaccessible tool, key, or person), and **Redirection** (partially displacing an incoming object rather than blocking it directly — extremely difficult, not something she masters early). Her fighting style should stay positional; the Stitch supports that, it doesn't replace it — and per Section 18.7/18.8, actual physical skill and Stitch mastery develop on two separate tracks, with the gap between them a live source of danger.

### 8.6 Mid-Phase Cost Progression and Partial Transit Failure

🟢 Beyond nosebleeds/blackouts, mid-phase repeated use produces early signs that something less recoverable is being spent: memories becoming harder to retrieve, familiar sensory associations weakening, emotional responses arriving muted, established habits briefly disappearing, System Traits fluctuating. Kasane should initially misattribute this to exhaustion, then stress, then damage — the horror is realizing later that rest doesn't restore all of it.

**Partial transit failure** (incomplete resolution) is one of the more dangerous mid-phase failure modes: a hand returning before the object it was holding, a foot exiting at the wrong angle, a weapon staying partly interactive with both layers, clothing catching on Ura geometry. Keep this rare and never slapstick — the disturbing possibility isn't gore, it's that the affected part temporarily stops behaving according to either world's rules (a hand can be physically intact and still feel wrong for hours, which reads as more unsettling than a clean injury).

### 8.7 Late Phase: Dual Presence and Pulling Through

🟢 Late-stage Kasane can maintain herself across both layers simultaneously — one person participating in two spatial states, not cloning. Possible effects: reacting to threats from either layer, anchoring with terrain present on only one side, carrying force or movement between sides, maintaining a seam long enough for deliberate extraction. At this point the Stitch stops reading as a strange Hunter ability and starts reading as authority over the distinction between worlds — which is exactly when the geopolitical fear (Section 8.11 below) becomes justified rather than paranoid.

**Pulling through** — deliberately pulling Ura matter (material, terrain, organisms, structures, conditions) into human reality without an existing Gate — is among the most dangerous applications and should never be casually reversible. Every large-scale use must raise real questions beyond the immediate scene: does it remain, does the human-side counterpart change, what happens to matter already occupying that space, are there sympathetic effects elsewhere (Section 6), does the Erosion notice, and — pointedly — has she just created a Gate by another name?

### 8.8 Cost Rules (extends the locked general Cost Philosophy)

🟢 Two standing rules for how permanent costs get assigned, not just what they are: **Never random for drama** — the cost should track duration, mass, distance, instability, and how much/many she's carrying through, even if the exact mechanism stays mysterious to the reader; the narrative logic should let a reader think "that was too much, something's going to be missing" without necessarily knowing what. **No clean exchange rate** — never "one memory equals fifteen seconds," since a legible tariff kills the horror; she may notice broad correlations, but sometimes pays more than expected, sometimes less, and sometimes doesn't recognize what's gone until much later.

### 8.9 The Erosion's Interest — Four Competing Interpretations (extends Section 11 and the Accident→Interest progression already established in Section 18.10)

🟢 None confirmed, all kept live: **Threat** (she can interfere with weak points and close seams, so it wants her eliminated); **Tool** (she can do from the human side what it's spent centuries attempting from the Ura side, so it wants her alive); **Missing Component** (her existence might indicate some condition its work requires, not yet understood); **Curiosity** (it may not have concepts like enemy/ally/tool at all — she may simply be the first unexpected thing in a very long time). Do not confirm any of these early.

### 8.10 Can the Stitch Seal? (extends the existing "she could be the Erosion's best weapon or its perfect tool" ambiguity)

🟡 Potentially, and this is one of the power's most important long-term directions — but sealing is not simply opening in reverse. Opening comes naturally because her anomaly is fundamentally permissive (the boundary lets her violate separation); closing would require understanding what separation actually *is*, which should demand knowledge rather than raw power, and should stay harder than opening for a long time.

### 8.11 Regional Fear, Reasoned Through (extends Sections 12, 13.1–13.3, 13.4, and 13.7's Meridian material — reuses each region's already-established institutional logic rather than adding new lore)

- **Yamashiro** (three levels, per Section 12): *Practical* — an uncontrolled breach hazard with no known Gate. *Political* — no Clan owns her, no lineage explains her, no license covers her. *Theological* — the Clans' legitimacy depends on boundaries being divinely meaningful; her existence says the boundary can simply be bypassed, which is doctrinally offensive independent of anything she's done.
- **Haejin:** a Gate with no fixed location breaks the assumptions behind contracts, extraction rights, insurance, and territory (Section 13.1) — their first question is less "can she kill us" than **"who owns what she opens."**
- **Bailian:** Null Passage threatens the premise that Ura access is divinely mediated (Section 13.2) — if she can cross without ordination, either she's uniquely blessed, she's blasphemous, or Communion doctrine is incomplete, and every answer is politically dangerous to someone.
- **Erkhaan:** a mobile breach is a strategic nightmare for a doctrine built on Incursion Points having fixed locations (Section 13.3) — command may end up classifying her less as a Hunter than as **strategic terrain with legs.**
- **The Harrowed:** the least philosophically troubled by her (Section 13.4) — the real questions are *does it work*, *what does it cost*, and, if those answers are acceptable, *can it get us home.*
- **The Meridian Network** (Section 13.7): her ability undermines the premise of secure location itself, which is Meridian's entire security model (biometric tracking, predictive movement, controlled infrastructure) — she doesn't need to beat the system, she invalidates the question the system depends on: *where is she?*

### 8.12 Anti-Power-Creep Rules: Travel, Locks, Gates, Passengers, Creatures, Death

🟢 Standing constraints for any future scene:
- **Must not make travel irrelevant:** unknown Ura geography, time instability, escalating cost, Erosion attention, and physical exhaustion mean shortcuts become possible but unlimited fast travel doesn't — extends Section 13.12's map-scale rule directly. Long-distance uses later in the saga should read as expeditions or desperate gambles, never as transportation infrastructure.
- **Must not make locks irrelevant:** bypassing a barrier still requires knowing what's on the other side, what occupies the Ura equivalent, whether resolving back is safe, and whether the location is warded — a door is often genuinely safer than the shortcut.
- **Must not solve every Gate crisis:** a Gate incident can involve hundreds of creatures, evacuation, structural collapse, and Echo effects at once — she can manipulate one important element and still need fighters, healers, surveyors, and rescue workers, protecting the ensemble structure (Section 17).
- **Carrying passengers:** moving another person is substantially harder than moving herself — impossible early, an emergency partial movement (a hand, an arm) mid-phase, possible-but-dangerous-and-expensive late. A struggling passenger makes everything worse; trust becomes mechanically relevant. 🟡 Marked vs. unmarked passengers may behave differently (a Marked passenger already partially participates in both layers, which could cut either way) — left as a research question to resolve only when Kasane actually attempts it.
- **Creature interaction** (extends Section 10's ecology): Ura-native creatures don't react to her uniformly — some flee, some investigate, some don't register her at all, some treat her as prey, some as neither prey nor predator. Threshold organisms specifically should read as especially strange around her, since they already live near the seam — an early, unexplained clue that the Stitch changes what category of thing the environment considers her to be.
- **Must not become a resurrection mechanic:** death stays meaningful. If Echoes or Ura phenomena resemble the dead, that's ambiguity, not an afterlife system — she should eventually be tempted to confuse *something remains* with *the person remains*, and those are not the same claim.

### 8.13 Visual and Audio Language (craft calibration, extends Section 1's prose-mechanics rules)

🟢 Visual: restrained, never a glowing portal except possibly at a truly late-stage catastrophic use. Early — misaligned edges, an object fractionally doubled, depth behaving incorrectly, shadows disagreeing. Mid — short seams in space, terrain glimpsed through impossible angles, Kasane briefly occupying two positions at once. Late — the environment itself disagreeing about which world is present. The effect should scale because the *concept* is scaling, not because the color palette gets brighter. Audio: sound should often signal the Stitch before light does — sudden absence of ambient noise, one sound heard twice from different directions, impact arriving before visible contact, a pressure-pop in nearby Marked ears, an animal reacting first. Avoid generic magical hums; use physical wrongness.

### 8.15 Final Writing Rules — The Stitch (extends Section 18.22's Kasane-specific rules)

**Always:** keep the ability spatial and physical; preserve uncertainty; make her experiment rather than intuit everything; keep mundane tools relevant; tie greater capability to better technique, not just more power; make local Ura geography matter; make witnesses matter; let failure be social/political/physical/personal in turn; track costs concretely; keep Erosion attention an escalating pressure; keep other characters necessary.

**Never:** make it generic teleportation; make it a clean inventory system; let her spam it; give her unexplained new functions during a climax; use nosebleeds forever as the only cost; leave permanent costs vague; treat memory loss as interchangeable tragedy currency; let the Stitch replace actual combat training; let it trivialize distance, prisons, Gates, or borders; let the System suddenly understand it; explain the late-saga ontology (Section 5H/8H) early; make the Erosion immediately conversational; turn Kasane into a chosen one.
