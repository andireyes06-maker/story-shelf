## Locked Characters

### Genzou — Pre-Marking Tie (charcoal & timber factor)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Mid-40s, valley-town merchant class, unmarked, no rank.

**Narrative function:** The strongest connection to Kasane's ordinary life before Chapter 1, and the site of her Arc 1 intellectual-flaw failure (see below).

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Negotiating a multi-season supply contract with a Sword Provinces forge-house ahead of Rite-driven demand, protecting his margin against a cheaper rival buyer who's been undercutting him for two seasons. Responsible for a small transport crew and several other highland suppliers' credit.

**Competence:** Reads buyers, market timing, and multi-party negotiation better than Kasane — specifically, he understands how rumor moves through trade networks faster than she, isolated on her mountain, would expect. A domain where he is straightforwardly right and she is wrong.

**Blind spot:** Assumes economic logic governs everyone's choices, including his own; slow to notice when his own behavior is running on something other than the ledger.

**Relationship to Kasane:** Years-long supplier relationship — arranged her transport, negotiated her prices, floated her credit once during a bad season, passed along road and market rumor.

**What he wants from Kasane:** His reliable supplier back, full stop — not a legend. Underneath that, quietly, a chance not to repeat an old failure (he once dropped a struggling supplier for business reasons, and it still bothers him).

**What Kasane initially thinks about him:** Neutral ground — the one relationship in her life that runs entirely on things she's actually good at.

**What he misunderstands about Kasane:** Reads her distress as fear of losing income or reputation — a merchant's read of a merchant's problem. Doesn't grasp that what's actually gone is her ability to predict her own life a week ahead.

**What Kasane misunderstands about him (Arc 1 flaw-cost mechanism — approved location):** She assumes his continued support has always been simple personal loyalty, and — critically — she overextends that assumption into a prediction: that he'd rather be spared association with her case once it becomes an institutional liability. Acting on that prediction, she commits to a costlier, less trustworthy alternative arrangement instead of asking him for help she actually needs, specifically to protect him from the risk. He refuses to be protected — he insists on providing the help anyway, publicly, on his own account, because quietly letting her go around him would repeat the old failure he's never made peace with. **Cost to Kasane:** because she'd already committed to the alternative arrangement before his intervention, the two now visibly overlap for the same purpose — a discrepancy that hands whoever is already reviewing her case (Aya, or a Clan-affiliated observer) a second, harder-to-explain thread where there should have been none. She loses the single, clean account of her resources and movements she needed. This is not a case of missing information — she had full, accurate evidence of his pattern and was still wrong, because she treated a reasonable prediction as certain enough to make a real commitment before testing it.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** Whether her safety is better served by staying legible and transactional (his instinct) or illegible (hers) — unresolved regardless of trust.

**Independent life/problem:** The rival-buyer margin war, running whether or not Kasane exists.

**Connections to other cast members:** Ongoing supply/credit relationship with Suzu's shrine (fuel, running account). Impersonal, indirect economic tie to Renji via Akaishi's reconstruction-era demand for timber/charcoal.

**Voice notes:** Talks in units, prices, conditional clauses. Shows care through arrangements, not words. Angry — repeats a specific number, more clipped each time. Afraid — oddly formal and polite.

**Arc movement:** Starts warm, transactional, faintly proprietary. Institutional attention crowds out his access through the middle movements; the flaw-cost incident (above) tests trust badly. Possible end-direction — an inversion where Kasane starts checking on him rather than the reverse.

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Unmarked; no perception of the Ura. His evidence about Kasane's situation is ordinary-world only — behavior, rumor, the visible eye, disrupted supply.

**First likely appearance:** Movement I.

**Open decisions:** Identity/fate of the previously dropped supplier; whether he has dependents who'd be put at risk by public association with her; the exact scene in which the flaw-cost incident occurs (a chapter-sequencing decision, not a casting one).

### Aya — Institutional Handler / Administrator (Tsukishiro intelligencer)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Late 20s, Tsukishiro intelligencer, unmarked, no Hunter rank.

**Narrative function:** Embodies Yamashiro's need to classify what happened to Kasane. Begins adversarial/assessing, becomes genuinely invested over the middle movements.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Competing for a permanent seat in Tsukishiro's inner information circle; running her own minor informant network; in standing, pre-existing tension with a superior who embodies the clan's older hoard-everything culture, over when (or whether) to escalate information rather than sit on it.

**Competence:** Institutional and political subtext reading; procedural case-building — what counts as evidence, what must be documented, what triggers escalation.

**Blind spot:** Reads Kasane's calm as trained composure — a mask — when it's coping, and keeps waiting for a tell that isn't coming.

**Relationship to Kasane:** Assigned once the registration problem becomes visible (Movement II).

**What she wants from Kasane:** A classification she can defend professionally — and, personally, given her frustration with her own clan's culture, to be the one who does something *useful* with the case rather than filing it away.

**What Kasane initially thinks about her:** An interrogator wearing patience as a technique.

**What she misunderstands about Kasane:** The composure-as-mask error above.

**What Kasane misunderstands about her:** Reads Aya as a simple extension of Tsukishiro's institutional interest, underestimating that Aya's personal frustration with her own clan's hoarding culture could make her a genuine, if limited, ally.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** Kasane does not oppose classification in principle — she wants the thing that happened to her to acquire rules, because a thing with rules can be worked around. Her live, ongoing disagreement with Aya is about what a given classification would *justify* (surveillance, restriction, a Clan's claim) when the evidence doesn't yet support it — not whether documentation should exist at all. Aya continues to believe classification is necessary for everyone's safety, Kasane included; this doesn't fully resolve just because they come to trust each other personally.

**Independent life/problem:** The escalate-or-hoard tension with her superior, running independent of Kasane's case.

**Connections to other cast members:** Past joint incident-report work with Isato, including a standing, unresolved professional disagreement over how that prior incident was classified. An ordinary periodic administrative review — Tsukishiro's routine cross-check of supply, registration, and personnel records across all Clans — puts her in occasional, mundane contact with Renji, whose post is due for review around the same time; no misconduct implied on either side.

**Voice notes:** Clipped, interrogative by habit. Long, deliberate pauses. Angry — retreats further into correct procedural language. Afraid (rare) — uncharacteristically direct.

**Arc movement:** Starts adversarial, assessing. Personal investment grows alongside real professional risk. Possible end-direction — genuine personal trust alongside continued, real disagreement about what documentation should authorize.

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Unmarked — cannot personally verify Stitch or Ura phenomena. First-hand evidence available to her: the visible eye anomaly, physical/documentary evidence, witness testimony, Kasane's own behavior. Not first-hand: anything about System text or Stitch mechanics, which reach her only as Kasane's report or a Marked assessor's account, filtered through her own interpretation — she has no direct access to what the System says to Kasane. Her exact appointing authority, and who ultimately signs off on any classification, remain open; jurisdiction over Kasane's case is itself an unresolved question (`Bible/01_WORLD_BIBLE.md` §12.3), and this cast file doesn't invent a resolution to it. The Rite raises public and political attention on the case; no absolute procedural deadline is asserted.

**First likely appearance:** Movement II.

**Open decisions:** Whether her tension with her superior surfaces on-page; who, if anyone, is ever established as the deciding authority over Kasane's classification.

### Isato — Veteran Hunter (Kuromori Warden)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Early-to-mid 50s, Kuromori Warden, **C-rank**, Marked.

**Narrative function:** Mentor without sentimentality; represents professional competence and collective safety rather than Kasane's personal autonomy.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Holding an understrength team together and competing against a rival Kuromori field commander for resources and reputation ahead of the Rite's Evaluation function — a competition that predates Kasane.

**Competence:** Combat, threat assessment, reading Marked instability in the field, telling ordinary Gate behavior from abnormal.

**Blind spot:** Conflates "needs more supervised practice" (a training-phase judgment) with "needs to stop hesitating" (an execution-phase judgment) — sound doctrine in general, misapplied to Kasane, whose actual bottleneck is epistemic caution about an unprecedented ability, not combat hesitation.

**Why C-rank, not B:** A Gate-Lord (B) is regional-power tier, carrying political weight whether he wants it or not — that seniority doesn't plausibly spend recurring personal time on one unregistered individual's assessment; that gets delegated. A Warden (C) — full independent license, the functional working rank — is senior enough to be credible and is the level actually assigned this kind of hands-on casework.

**Relationship to Kasane:** Enters Movement III, tasked with the practical question Kuromori cares about: is she a usable asset or a liability.

**What he wants from Kasane:** Usable competence — safely, where "safely" is the loaded word given his history.

**What Kasane initially thinks about him:** Another undertrained-body-someone's-about-to-get-killed-rushing authority figure.

**What he misunderstands about Kasane:** Reads her methodical caution as hesitation to commit and pushes her toward decisive action — the wrong pressure for her actual method.

**What Kasane misunderstands about him:** Reads his bluntness as plain professional indifference, missing that it's built from a specific old failure: a subordinate he once pushed toward early advancement by making the same category error, who died in a Gate incident she wasn't ready for.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect — but can genuinely shift:** Over time he can come to recognize her caution as a valid discipline rather than a deficiency. This shouldn't resolve for free, but isn't permanently closed either — earning it should cost him revising something he was certain of.

**Independent life/problem:** The rival-commander competition; the old guilt.

**Connections to other cast members:** Past joint incident-report work with Aya.

**Voice notes:** Economical, imperative. Teaches by demonstration ("Watch," never "let me explain"). Dry humor as a rare, deliberate signal of approval. Angry — goes quiet and still. Afraid (rare, extremity only) — hyper-procedural.

**Arc movement:** Starts blunt, assessing. His old guilt gets directly tested by her risk profile. Possible end-direction — earned professional respect, without requiring full personal warmth.

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Marked — one of the few cast members with genuine first-hand access to Ura-adjacent evidence and combat capability.

**First likely appearance:** Movement III.

**Open decisions:** Whether the dead trainee is shown on-page or named; identity of the rival Kuromori commander.

### Renji — Clan Insider / Rival (Akaishi minor scion)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Late 20s, Akaishi minor scion (cousin of the heir apparent), Marked, modest rank if any (❓ mechanical proposal, deliberately not his primary source of standing).

**Narrative function:** Gives Kasane a recurring personal relationship with hereditary Yamashiro power. Folds the Clan-heir and rival functions into one figure.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Competing against another Akaishi relative for a specific administrative appointment ahead of the Rite's Public Ceremony. Oversees a real logistics function in Akaishi's rebuilding territory, with actual staff and dependent households — stakes that predate Kasane.

**Competence:** Political timing; making a gesture land publicly without reading as cynical.

**Blind spot:** Assumes all behavior — including his own — is legible performance, because that's been true of nearly everyone in his experience.

**Relationship to Kasane:** His stake in the hidden-blood theory gives him a concrete, personal reason to seek her out repeatedly.

**What he wants from Kasane:** To be seen — publicly, unambiguously — as tied to her, which would help him against his internal rival.

**What Kasane initially thinks about him:** A threat dressed as an ally.

**What he misunderstands about Kasane:** Reads her refusals as negotiating posture and keeps escalating what he offers, mistaking retreat for holding out.

**What Kasane misunderstands about him:** Reads him as performing self-interest at all times, underestimating that his stakes are also about survival inside his own family.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** He can be substantively right that her insistence on staying unaligned with every faction isn't obviously safer than choosing one — Akaishi backing would give her real protection she currently lacks, and her neutrality can read as naive about her actual exposure. This is a defensible position, not manipulation dressed as concern, and it doesn't resolve just because she comes to understand him better. Understanding him better does not, by itself, give her reason to trust his judgment, discretion, or reliability — those stay separate questions. (Note: her protection is not solely dependent on Renji/Akaishi — Isato and Aya each offer other, non-hereditary forms of practical help.)

**Independent life/problem:** The internal-Akaishi appointment competition.

**Connections to other cast members:** Impersonal, indirect economic tie to Genzou via Akaishi's reconstruction-era demand. Ordinary periodic administrative review puts him in occasional contact with Aya (see her entry).

**Voice notes:** Performative, quick, rhetorically complete even under pressure. Angry — more charming, not less, a tell that something's wrong. Under real fear or humiliation, the performance drops to flat, plain sentences.

**Arc movement:** Starts as overt courtship/claiming pressure. A costly personal choice damages trust in a specific, nameable way. Possible end-direction — a harder-won, still partly unresolved relationship, not a clean thaw.

**Romance:** Not proposed. No distinct narrative reason has emerged, and the friction here doesn't constitute one on its own.

**First likely appearance:** Movement II, escalating through Movement III.

**Open decisions:** Identity of the internal-Akaishi rival relative.

### Suzu — Shrine Authority (Shrine Marches caretaker-priestess)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Mid-30s, caretaker-priestess of a shrine in the Shrine Marches, trained at and periodically answerable to Motoi's priesthood, unmarked.

**Narrative function:** Represents lived Yamashiro religion — actual ritual practice and community interpretation, not exposition.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Preparing her shrine's part in the Rite's Consecration function; maintaining ordinary pastoral duties (funerals, blessings, travel rites) for a real congregation.

**Competence:** Pastoral and emotional care; ritual/liturgical knowledge; community memory. Within her ordinary duties she's an unusually attentive record-keeper (which stones draw which reactions, which rites feel like they're doing something versus rote) — background competence, not a private investigation.

**Blind spot:** Prioritizes her congregation's stability over telling an uncomfortable truth at the wrong moment, even to Kasane.

**Relationship to Kasane:** A shrine caretaker with a real, functioning tie to Motoi's priesthood; her territory likely overlaps Kasane's own.

**What she wants from Kasane:** Her actual, duty-arising need is to know how to fold Kasane's case into ordinary pastoral and consecration practice — does an anomalous Marking require an altered blessing, a special rite, exclusion from a normal one? A real question her role requires her to answer, not a wish for a research subject.

**What Kasane initially thinks about her:** Harmless. Possibly a comfort.

**What she misunderstands about Kasane:** Assumes the dual eyes are a sign meant to be read, offering interpretation where Kasane wants a diagnosis.

**What Kasane misunderstands about her:** Following her general habit of filing quiet, ordinary-seeming people as harmless, underestimates that Suzu's calm is effortful and that she's genuinely weighing a real loyalty conflict, not simply being serene.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** Suzu wants to interpret meaning; Kasane wants facts — unresolved regardless of affection.

**Named, unresolved tension (not smoothed over):** She offers Kasane something that feels unassessed — pastoral space, not bureaucratic scrutiny — while structurally she does answer to Motoi and could be asked, by Motoi or by Clan pressure, to report what she's observed. No formal confidentiality rule is invented to settle this; it stays live.

**Independent life/problem:** The pastoral/Consecration workload; the Motoi-loyalty-vs-disclosure tension.

**Connections to other cast members:** Ongoing supply/credit relationship with Genzou (shrine fuel).

**Voice notes:** Soft, unhurried, answers direct questions with an image or story rather than a flat statement — deliberate, not muddled. Comfortable with long silence. Angry (rare) — very precise, quotes ritual formula pointedly. Afraid — over-performs calm for others.

**Arc movement:** Starts gentle, semi-familiar. Her pastoral duty and her loyalty to Motoi come under real tension over what, if anything, to disclose. Possible end-direction — a costly disclosure choice, not simply "gained faith" or "lost faith."

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Unmarked — no perception of the Ura, no privileged access to the Erosion's causes, Stitch ontology, or the objective truth of any sacred boundary. Everything she knows is testimony, folk pattern, and observed behavior.

**First likely appearance:** Not yet established — plausibly Movement II or III.

**Open decisions:** Whether she ultimately reports anything upward; how much of her record-keeping predates Chapter 1.

### Moritaka — Antagonist (Shirogane Gate-Lord)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Late 50s, Shirogane Gate-Lord, **B-rank**, Marked.

**Narrative function:** Personifies Shirogane's institutional interest — contain and classify (`Bible/01_WORLD_BIBLE.md` §12) — the way Aya personifies Tsukishiro's and Isato personifies Kuromori's. Gives an already-planned Movement III consequence (Chapter 8's autonomy restriction) a face rather than a faceless faction.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Managing Shirogane's declining Gate-response readiness from inside the Clan's old-guard/pragmatist split (`Bible/01_WORLD_BIBLE.md` §12) — old-guard by genuine conviction, not just faction loyalty, and privately aware of how thin the pragmatists' patience is wearing after eleven Shinken-less years.

**Competence:** Reading institutional risk before it escalates into open incident — a real, demonstrated skill, not a pretext.

**Blind spot:** Conflates control with safety. Applies his genuine skill at spotting institutional risk to Kasane specifically and concludes that restricting her is what reduces the danger, missing that her ongoing cooperation — not any Clan's oversight — is what has actually been containing the risk so far.

**Why Gate-Lord, not Warden:** Kuromori and Tsukishiro dispatched working-level specialists because their stakes (practical value, information) are routine casework, credibly delegated. Shirogane's stake is different in kind — for the oldest, most conservative Clan, anxious about its own relevance without a living Shinken, an unregistered anomaly reads as Clan-level political business, not administrative casework. Sending a Gate-Lord (B) signals the old guard is handling this at the top.

**Relationship to Kasane:** Enters once the per-Clan pressure map goes on-page (Movement III), tasked with the question Shirogane cares about: how fast this can be brought under formal control.

**What he wants from Kasane:** Not her destruction — her legibility. A fully restricted, fully documented quantity is an acceptable outcome to him; an autonomous unknown is not, regardless of her intentions or her record of cooperation.

**What Kasane initially thinks about him:** The first person in the process whose stated goal is narrower and colder than "figure out what happened to me" — someone measuring her against a policy, not a case file.

**What he misunderstands about Kasane:** Reads her calm competence as either performance or evidence she's more dangerous than she's shown — the same composure-as-mask error Aya makes (see her entry), pushed to a harder conclusion.

**What Kasane misunderstands about him:** Reads him as reaching for power or personal cruelty. He's reaching for a repeat of something specific he's never named to anyone — a past instance, elsewhere, of a Clan leaving an anomaly unrestricted too long and paying for it.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** He can come to recognize, without it changing his position, that she isn't acting in bad faith — and he can still be substantively right that unregulated, undocumented ability is genuinely dangerous in the abstract, even though restricting her isn't actually what reduces that danger. Neither side gets to be simply correct; this doesn't resolve just because either of them understands the other better.

**Independent life/problem:** The old-guard/pragmatist split within Shirogane, and his own unstated fear of presiding over a repeat of a past failure.

**Connections to other cast members:** Structural friction with Aya — her belief that documentation should exist collides with his belief in what documentation should be used to *justify*, sharpening her own live disagreement with Kasane (see her entry) rather than duplicating it. No direct prior relationship with Isato or Genzou; their paths cross only through Kasane's case.

**Voice notes:** Formal, procedural, comfortable citing policy rather than opinion. Never raises his voice — pressure shows as increased precision and shorter sentences. Angry — goes more formal, not less. Afraid (rare) — the only cast member whose fear reads as a specific old memory intruding rather than a present-tense reaction.

**Arc movement:** Starts as an institutional position with a face attached, escalates through Movement III to personally enact the autonomy-restriction consequence at its close. Possible end-direction — remains a genuine, unresolved obstacle rather than a redeemed or defeated antagonist; per the project's per-Clan design, he doesn't need to be right or wrong, only real.

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Marked, Gate-Lord rank — real first-hand combat/Gate-response experience, though no more direct access to Stitch mechanics than any other Marked-but-not-Kasane observer (`Bible/03_MAGIC_AND_SYSTEM.md`).

**First likely appearance:** Movement III (Chapter 5, when the per-Clan pressure map goes on-page), escalating through Chapter 8.

**Open decisions:** The specific past incident behind his fear (identity, Clan, whether it's ever named on-page); whether he appears again after Movement III or remains a Movement III–specific pressure.

### Noe — Peer (unaffiliated, Black Mountain Belt)

**Canon status:** 🟢 Approved. Early-to-mid 20s, Black Mountain Belt local, **E-rank (Traced)**, Marked.

**Narrative function:** The one relationship in Kasane's Arc 1 cast with no authority gradient in either direction — every other recurring relationship processes, judges, assesses, or has an institutional claim on her. Noe doesn't.

**Independent life, this month, without Kasane's Marking:** Working a low support role held for several years, stalled at Traced rank well past what competence alone would predict — a direct, lived instance of the world's own rule that support and information roles are chronically underranked. Managing an unrelated, ordinary problem of her own (a housing or debt situation tied to her stalled rank) that has nothing to do with Kasane and won't be solved by anything that happens to her.

**Competence:** Knows how to actually last inside the system at low rank long-term — which offices to avoid, which promises don't get kept, how to keep a file thin on purpose.

**Blind spot:** Has made peace with a ceiling by deciding not to want anything past it, and reflexively undersells how much worse a case can still get once real institutional attention — of the kind Kasane is now getting — is actually involved.

**Relationship to Kasane:** A loose pre-Marking acquaintance — same charcoal country, both known through Genzou's supplier network, never close — that becomes an actual friendship only after Kasane's Marking, once they're both people the system keeps a file on.

**What Kasane initially thinks about her:** Proof the system is survivable long-term.

**What Noe misunderstands about Kasane:** Assumes Kasane's case will eventually settle into the same ordinary bureaucratic obscurity most Marked cases do, and has no real framework for how fast and how far this one is already moving.

**What Kasane misunderstands about her:** Reads Noe's calm as evidence of a livable equilibrium. It's closer to resignation, arrived at the hard way, and it costs Noe something every time Kasane's case reminds her what she gave up wanting.

**Disagreement that survives growing respect:** Noe thinks staying small and undocumented is the only real protection available at low rank; Kasane's whole situation is a case study in that protection failing anyway — neither fully persuades the other, because they're arguing from genuinely different exposure levels.

**Independent life/problem:** Her own stalled rank and the ordinary debt/housing problem that comes with it, running whether or not Kasane's case exists.

**Connections to other cast members:** Indirect tie to Genzou via his supplier network (see his entry). No direct relationship with Aya, Isato, Renji, or Suzu — enters Kasane's orbit independently of the institutional cast.

**Voice notes:** Plain, unhurried, deflects seriousness with dry understatement. Angry — goes flatter and quieter, not louder. Afraid — over-explains ordinary logistics as a way of not discussing the actual fear.

**Arc movement:** Starts as an easy, low-stakes friendship — the one relationship that doesn't cost Kasane anything to maintain. Possible tension point later in the Arc: Noe's own stalled situation could be helped by proximity to Kasane's now-prominent case, creating a real, uncomfortable question about whether either of them would ever use the other that way.

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Marked, E-rank — genuine but limited first-hand Ura-adjacent experience; no political or institutional standing to leverage on Kasane's behalf.

**First likely appearance:** Movement III or IV — present around the assessment/trials process as a fellow low-rank figure, never as one of its authorities.

**Open decisions:** The specific nature of her stalled-rank problem (debt vs. housing vs. something else); whether the "proximity to a prominent case" tension point is used at all, and if so, how far.

**Competence:** Reads institutional desperation and denial accurately, in her own Clan and elsewhere, and doesn't flinch from naming it.

**Blind spot:** Wants Kasane to be the answer Karasu needs badly enough that she risks overstating, to her own leadership, what a bounded Contact/Knowledge-limited examination (`Bible/03_MAGIC_AND_SYSTEM.md` §8.3) could actually resolve.

Possible end-direction — left open, consistent with the Karasu Silence itself staying unresolved (`Bible/04_CONTINUITY_AND_MYSTERIES.md`).

**Authority/knowledge limits:** Unmarked. Does not know, and her leadership does not know, whether the Silence was a successful Erosion-interruption, a coincidence, or something worse (`Bible/04_CONTINUITY_AND_MYSTERIES.md`) — nothing about her characterization implies she or Karasu are close to that answer.

**First likely appearance:** Movement V (Chapter 12), consistent with the existing plan.

**Open decisions:** Whether she reappears after Chapter 12 or remains a Movement-V-specific presence; how explicitly her personal stake (people she trained with leaving) is shown on-page versus implied.
