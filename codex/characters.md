## KASANE — BASE PROFILE (original locked handoff)

## 5. ARC 1 PROTAGONIST — KASANE

**Identity**
- Name: Kasane (重 — layered, doubled). No clan surname. Known as Kasane of the Ash or simply Kasane.
- Gender / Age: Female, 26.
- Trade: Charcoal-burner (sumi-yaki). Works the mid-mountain zones above Yamashiro's valley towns — invisible to the clans above, irrelevant to the towns below. Alone for weeks at a time managing slow burns.
- Rank at story start: Newly Marked. Unregistered. Technically F-rank the moment she's processed. Her processing is being deliberately delayed.

**Why She Specifically**
The Erosion was working on a weak point in the Ura's membrane near her burn site in the mountains — part of its long, slow project to force the seam fully open. The Gate that opened and Marked her was an accidental byproduct of that working. The Marking reached through the weakened seam and grabbed the nearest human. That was her.

She is the Erosion's unintended consequence. It was trying to open the world. What it opened was her.

The System knows something is wrong with how she was Marked. It cannot say what. It keeps trying.

**Personality**
Dry pragmatist + craft-logic thinker. Charcoal burning is about controlled containment of a slow fire — you read it, you manage it, you do not fight it. Kasane applies this logic to everything, including increasingly catastrophic situations. The humor is in watching her treat Gate breaches and S-rank clan politics as containment problems.

Her primary edge — before the Stitch and separate from it — is observational intelligence. Running burns alone for weeks means learning to read terrain, weather, and the merchants who come to collect. She extrapolates people the same way she extrapolates weather patterns. She's usually right. She's also usually annoying about it.

How she handles rumor: Kasane treats a rumor the way she treats weather — not true/false, but a pattern with tells. Who benefits if this is believed. How many independent mouths it passed through before reaching her. Whether the details are suspiciously clean (real accounts are messy; manufactured ones are tidy). She's wrong sometimes, and wrong in a way that costs her, but her method is sound even when a specific read isn't. This becomes the reader's lens on every rumor, myth fragment, and secondhand report about the wider world (see Section 8 regional rumor characters, Section 9 mythology fragments).

She is not heroic. She is not cowardly. She is practical in ways the hunter system is poorly equipped to deal with.

**The Stitch — Her Power**
(See Section 7 for full mechanics.)

Kasane calls it the Stitch. Not a gift — an interruption. The Stitch is what the Erosion's working accidentally gave her when it weakened the seam near her burn site.

## KASANE — DEEP PROFILE

## SECTION 18 — KASANE: DEEP CHARACTER PROFILE

Cross-checked against your character-deep-dive source document. Where it restates already-locked material (her name/meaning, charcoal-burning trade, F-rank/unregistered start, the Erosion-accident origin of her Marking, the System's classification failure, rumor-evaluation method, saga-first-among-equals status), this section cross-references rather than repeats it. Everything below is genuinely new — physical presence, psychology, speech, relationship architecture, and explicit writing rules that didn't exist anywhere in the bible before.

### 18.1 Physical Presence

🟢 Shaped by labor, not combat training: medium height, lean, wiry, strong forearms and hands, excellent balance on uneven terrain, endurance from repetitive work — poor explosive athleticism next to a trained Hunter. She can haul, split, climb, and hold an awkward position for hours; she cannot win a straight sword exchange, outrun a trained Hunter, or absorb a heavy blow. This gap should stay visible even as she grows more capable — the Stitch changes what she can do, not what her body is.

Her hands matter specifically: callused, nicked, permanently stained in the creases. She reads other people's hands before their faces — whether someone actually works, trains, is pretending to be poorer than they are, has recently handled a weapon, or has ever done the labor they regulate. Hair dark, badly tied rather than styled, ends frequently singed. Clothing is practical and shows repair, not replacement, until it stops doing its job — when later given something expensive, her first instinct is to check whether she can move in it, where a knife could sit, whether the fabric survives sparks.

### 18.2 The Eyes — Visual Calibration (extends the locked dual-eye anomaly)

🟢 Restrained, not neon: at rest, a subtly altered iris tone and something slightly too reflective — familiar Marked people register the wrongness quickly. Under stress or Stitch use, the color intensifies, faint light develops beneath the iris, the shift may not land evenly between both eyes, and prolonged use can leave afterimages in her own vision. People look at her eyes before her face; she notices every time and rarely comments. This is calibration for how the anomaly should read on the page, not new lore.

### 18.3 System-Text Calibration for Her Anomaly (ties to Section 1.9 and Section 8's classification-failure material)

🟢 A concrete example of the System failing on her specifically, usable as reference alongside the existing ×47 example: `[MARKING CONFIRMED.]` → `[CLASSIFICATION PENDING.]` → `[ABILITY EXPRESSION DETECTED.]` → `[CATEGORY: —]` → `[RECALCULATING.]` → `[BOUNDARY INTERACTION: ILLEGAL STATE.]` — "illegal" in the mathematical sense (a condition that shouldn't exist), not the criminal one. The dark comedy is that human authorities later misread this as an accusation of wrongdoing rather than a math error — worth remembering as a specific, plantable misunderstanding for an institutional scene.

### 18.4 Craft-Logic Thinking, Worked Examples (extends the locked "containment, not combat" trait)

🟢 Where others ask *how do we kill it*, she asks *where is it trying to go*. Where others ask *how do we overpower this Gate*, she asks *what is feeding it*. In an escalating political argument, she asks *which part actually needs resolving before the rest burns down*. This reads as calm; it isn't — it's troubleshooting. Useful as a template for writing her dialogue in any high-stakes scene: her question is never the one about power, always the one about mechanism.

### 18.5 The Core Intellectual Flaw (new — extends her observational-intelligence trait, Section 5 old numbering)

🟢 Her pattern-reading works on terrain, weather, and incentives — but she initially assumes people are as consistent as those systems. People can know something is foolish and do it anyway: love someone they distrust, choose pride over survival, act against their own stated goals. This means Kasane's reads can be exactly right about a person's incentives and still wrong about what they'll actually choose. This should genuinely cost her at least once in Arc 1 — not as a one-off humbling beat, but as the specific shape her intelligence's blind spot takes.

### 18.6 Voice: Humor, Speech Rhythm (extends Section 1's craft rulebook — character-specific application)

🟢 Her humor is understatement and practical comparison, never a quip register — she doesn't match other people's dramatic pitch. A clan lord invoking ancestral divine will gets measured against whether that blood lets him close a door; a "catastrophic spiritual calamity" gets measured against the building that's also, concretely, on fire. She isn't mocking danger — she has limited patience for impressive language while a practical problem sits unresolved. Speech-wise: short answers, literal observations, dry qualifications, and questions that reveal she's noticed something inconvenient. The angrier she is, the fewer unnecessary words she uses. Frightened, she talks through practical steps because naming tasks gives her something to hold — never generic panic-language (already a locked rule, Section 1.4; this is its concrete expression for her specifically).

### 18.7 Education and Material Skills — Boundaries

🟢 Functional literacy, arithmetic for trade, weights/measures, local geography, forestry, charcoal production, market habits, ordinary-citizen shrine custom — she has all of this and it's never treated as a gap to explain away. She does *not* have clan history, formal theology, Hunter doctrine, international geography, System terminology, elite etiquette, or foreign languages — these are genuine unknowns, not withheld-for-suspense gaps, and other characters should be able to tell her things in these areas without it reading as exposition-for-the-reader. Pre-Stitch material skills (axe, knife, climbing, fire-making, tool maintenance, weather/distance estimation, functioning sleep-deprived) should keep mattering after she gains combat ability, not get quietly retired.

### 18.8 Combat Philosophy

🟢 Not "how do I win" but "how do I avoid being killed by something stronger": identify where it can move, what it notices, what it can't easily reach, put something inconvenient between herself and it, leave. "Kill it" becomes realistic only later. Even once more capable, she prefers terrain, leverage, positioning, traps, denying movement, and forcing bad angles over a fair fight — her stated reasoning: if it's fair, something has already gone wrong.

### 18.9 Fear, Anger, Kindness — Physical Presentation Only (extends Section 1.4's "show, don't summarize" rule)

🟢 Fear: dry mouth, trembling fingers, shallow breathing, fixation on small practical details, occasionally vomiting after danger passes — she doesn't become fearless as she grows stronger, she becomes better at functioning while afraid. Anger: quiet, not theatrical — she becomes difficult to move, not loud. She tolerates arrogance (she's spent her life around merchants) but not arrogance that creates work for everyone else. Kindness: practical and usually silent — repairing something without mentioning it, taking the colder sleeping spot because she tolerates it better, moving someone out of bad weather — she dislikes gratitude for what she considers obvious, which can read to others as caring less than she does.

### 18.10 Authority, the System, the Ura, the Erosion — Four Relationships That Evolve

- *Authority:* Not rebellious by default — she follows rules whose purpose she can identify and drops ones she can't once obedience turns dangerous. This produces **conditional cooperation**, which is harder for institutions to manage than either obedience or rebellion would be.
- *The System:* Neither trusted nor distrusted at first — treated as an instrument, like smoke off a kiln. If two readings contradict, she assumes the instrument or the thing it's measuring is wrong, not that one reading must be divinely true. This attitude — that the System can be confused — becomes important later, since most Hunters treat it as authoritative.
- *The Ura:* Fear → professional curiosity → recognition. She begins noticing patterns in Ura terrain the way she once read airflow through a burn — unsettling specifically because it implies some part of the Ura is becoming *legible* to her, not just survivable.
- *The Erosion:* 🟢 A concrete five-stage progression, extending Section 11's "it has noticed Stitch use" material: **Accident → Anomaly → Observation → Recognition → Interest.** Do not make it a chatty personal villain early — the genuinely frightening beat isn't when it speaks to her, it's when she realizes something that used to treat humanity like landscape has started distinguishing her from the rest of it.

### 18.11 Relationship to Her Own Power

🟢 She actively resists the romanticizing language other people reach for (miraculous, cursed, forbidden, divine, revolutionary) — "the Stitch" because a stitch joins two things through a puncture, and because she needed something shorter than whatever the System was trying to call it. Regards it as useful, dangerous, badly understood, and hers whether she likes that or not. The power should never become her personality — a standing check against any scene that starts treating the Stitch as her defining trait rather than one fact about her.

### 18.12 Objects, Food, Habits (texture, reusable across scenes)

🟢 Recurring possessions, none secretly magical, all important because she uses them: her hatchet (old, maintained, carried long after better weapons are available); a **burn ledger** — a practical notebook of charcoal yields, weather, buyers, debts, kiln timing, whose later pages slowly accumulate Gate observations, rumors, and System inconsistencies, making it one of the earliest actual records of Null Passage; a smoke-marked fire cloth/scarf; a whetstone, used often enough that sharpening becomes a thinking-activity. Food: filling, simple, portable — stews, rice, preserved vegetables, grilled river fish; court cuisine initially frustrates her mainly because there isn't enough of it, and feeding her before hard questions is a real, non-gimmicky way to earn goodwill. Habits: testing wind with a damp finger, smelling unfamiliar materials, tapping wood/stone for density, counting exits without deciding to, checking knots other people tied, watching smoke while thinking.

### 18.13 How Each Faction Misreads Her

🟢 Clan aristocrats read her silence as ignorance; Hunters initially read her caution as cowardice; priests read her anomaly through their own theology rather than asking her; merchants recognize her bargaining instincts fastest of anyone; ordinary workers understand her almost immediately; high-ranked Marked individuals find her lack of awe either refreshing or infuriating depending on their own insecurity; the System simply cannot decide what she is, and increasingly, neither can she.

### 18.14 Core Contradictions (synthesis)

🟢 Practical, but possesses an impossible ability. Observant, but misreads emotion (Section 18.5). Independent, but shaped by ordinary community. Cautious, but repeatedly walks toward danger when leaving it alone would be worse. Socially unimportant, but geopolitically significant. Not ambitious, but unable to return to anonymity. Good at containment, while holding the ability most capable of breaking it — this last one is the central contradiction the character runs on.

### 18.15 Arc 1 Want / Need / Character Movement

- **Internal want:** to understand what happened well enough that it stops disrupting her life — deliberately modest; she wants the strange thing to acquire rules, because a thing with rules can be worked around.
- **External want, in order:** survive the Marking → reach civilization → avoid being treated as a Gate-creature → learn what happened to her eyes → complete registration → understand the Stitch → stop powerful people from deciding her future without her.
- **Need:** to learn that some problems can't be contained from outside them — her instinct is always to manage the fire at a remove; Arc 1 forces the recognition that she's already inside the kiln.
- **Movement, beat by beat:** *Beginning* — "something happened to me, I need to understand it and get my life back." *Early middle* — "the people who claim to understand this don't understand it either." *Late middle* — "some of them know that and are hiding it." *Climax* — forced into a moment where using the Stitch openly forecloses anonymity; she uses it anyway, not from accepting destiny but because not using it produces an outcome she can't accept. *End* — not heroic, **committed**: she's lost the possibility of returning to who she was, and gained the start of her own understanding of the world in its place.

### 18.16 Long-Term Arc and Ensemble Role (extends Section 17's saga architecture)

🟢 Saga-wide progression: **ordinary → implicated → responsible → connected** — not "ordinary → strongest." She already knows how to stand alone; the real long-arc challenge is learning that relying on other people doesn't weaken the structure, the way a kiln holds because every stone carries part of the weight. As first among equals (locked, Section 17), later protagonists must disagree with her, solve problems she can't, keep relationships independent of her, and develop while she's off-page — her eventual found-family should read as people whose lives collided, not followers she collected.

### 18.17 Romance (if it happens)

🟡 Not required for her characterization. If it develops: grows from repeated trust, practical compatibility, vulnerability, and disagreement — not conventional courtship, which she's unlikely to respond to. Status displays land poorly; competence gets her attention, reliability keeps it (noticing she's exhausted and quietly taking over a task lands harder than a grand gesture). No timeline implied or needed.

### 18.18 Core Relationship Archetypes for Arc 1 (casting slots, not named characters — consistent with Section 17's discipline against inventing names prematurely)

🟢 The Institutional Handler (assigned to process/monitor her, begins adversarial, becomes invested); the Clan Heir (raised inside everything she stands outside of — neither should simply envy the other); the Veteran Hunter (understands exactly how dangerous her inexperience is; mentor without sentimentality); the Ordinary Civilian (a pre-Marking tie, necessary so the life she's losing stays a felt loss, not an abstraction); the Priest/Shrine Keeper (positioned to recognize the theological catastrophe her Marking represents — maps naturally onto Motoi's priesthood, Section 12); the Rival (not necessarily stronger — someone whose worldview clashes with her containment mentality specifically).

### 18.19 Class, Power, and What She Fears Becoming

🟢 She retains labor-awareness even inside powerful circles — noticing who carries the trays while nobles argue, who cleans a Gate site afterward, whose taxes fund a heroic ceremony. As her own power grows, the live danger is that she could start forgetting to notice these things too; the story should periodically test this rather than assume it's permanently solved. Her deepest long-term fear isn't death (a concrete danger she already understands) — it's becoming something that no longer finds ordinary human concerns relevant, the same failure mode she increasingly recognizes in powerful people who treat casualties as numbers and civilians as acceptable loss. 🟡 This parallels the Erosion directly: it may not hate humanity, it may simply operate at a scale where individuals stop registering — which is why what keeps Kasane human isn't willpower or destiny, it's continued attention to small things (meals, names, repairs, weather, promises, people), and why that detail is thematically load-bearing, not just characterization texture.

### 18.20 What Makes Her Dangerous (ties to Section 11's Erosion material and Section 14's institutional-legitimacy material)

🟢 Not raw power — the combination of an ability that violates a foundational rule, patience, observational intelligence, willingness to test assumptions, and little emotional investment in preserving institutional myths. Where someone else might panic on discovering a sacred rule is false, her sequence is *how long has it been false* → *who already knows* → *what else depends on everyone believing it*. That sequence is what makes her dangerous to every institution built on certainty, not just to whatever she's fighting in a given scene.

### 18.21 Kasane in One Sentence, and the Thematic Question

🟢 **"Kasane is a charcoal-burner who spent her life learning how to keep slow fires contained, then accidentally became the one person in the world capable of opening the boundary everyone else is trying to keep closed."** Her recurring thematic question: *if you can cross a boundary, does that give you the right to break it* — and beneath it, *if the world keeps placing responsibility in your hands without your consent, at what point does refusing to choose become a choice of its own?* This should never resolve to one permanent answer; the answer should shift as the scale of what she can affect grows, carrying from Arc 1 into the saga (Section 17).

### 18.22 Character Writing Rules — Kasane-Specific (extends Section 1's general craft rulebook)

**Do:** let her notice practical details first; allow her to misread emotional situations (Section 18.5); keep her competence specific, not universal; show fear physically (18.9); let her make mistakes; preserve her working-class habits even after she's powerful; keep humor dry and understated under pressure; allow silence; let her ask inconveniently precise questions; make her earn combat competence; keep the Stitch costly (locked, Section 8); make relationships increasingly important over the saga (18.16).

**Don't:** turn her into a quip machine; make her instantly politically brilliant; make every prediction correct; give her hidden noble ancestry to justify her importance (no such reveal is planned or wanted); reveal she was secretly destined; make everyone immediately fascinated by her; write her as emotionless; confuse her pragmatism with cruelty; let the Stitch solve every problem; let growing power quietly erase her mundane limitations; make her the strongest person in every scene; let other protagonists orbit her without independent lives of their own (locked, Section 17's ensemble discipline).

**Continuity note:** This section is additive to the existing Kasane material (old-numbering Section 5, and Section 8's Stitch mechanics) — nothing here overrides either; 18.22's rules should be checked against any future Kasane-POV draft the same way Section 1's general rulebook already is.
